# IELTS Speaking — Band 8–9 Master Book

**The official descriptors. What examiners listen for. How to get there.**

> Target: Band 8–9. Based on the official IELTS Speaking Band Descriptors and Key Assessment
> Criteria (ielts.org), the official Speaking format, and established IELTS teaching practice.
> Verified November 2026. There is **one Speaking test** for Academic and General Training.

---

## How to use this book

1. **Part 1** — the rules and the exact Band 8/9 descriptors. This is your target definition.
2. **Part 2** — one chapter per criterion. Each criterion is 25% of your mark.
3. **Part 3** — the three parts, with annotated model answers and expansion methods.
4. **Part 4** — the toolkit: fillers, paraphrase, pronunciation, traps.
5. **Part 5** — practice system and a 4-week plan.
6. **Final chapter** — specifically how to move from 8 to 9.

**Golden rule:** learn from model answers, never memorise them. The examiner is testing your
ability to speak naturally, not to recite.

# PART 1 — KNOW THE TEST

## Chapter 1 — Format

- **Face-to-face interview with an examiner** (or video call). **The test is recorded.**
- **11–14 minutes**, three parts. The examiner controls the timing.
- The examiner who asks the questions decides your score **after the test ends**.
- You are **not marked per part** — the score reflects your performance across the whole test.
- Topics are recycled. The examiner isn't interested in your knowledge — topics are a vehicle
  to showcase language.
- The test is **informal**; informal language is normal and appropriate.

| Part | Name | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction and interview | 4–5 min | ~12 questions on ~3 everyday topics |
| 2 | Long turn | 3–4 min | Cue card, 1 min prep, speak 1–2 min, then 1–2 rounding questions |
| 3 | Discussion | 4–5 min | ~5–6 deeper questions on a related topic; examiner may challenge you |

A recording exists so you can request a **remark by a senior examiner** if you think the mark was unfair.

## Chapter 2 — The four criteria and the scoring

Each criterion carries **25%** of your mark:

1. **Fluency and Coherence**
2. **Lexical Resource**
3. **Grammatical Range and Accuracy**
4. **Pronunciation**

Your final band is calculated from your **average performance across all three parts**, not
from any single answer. There is no raw-score conversion in Speaking.

## Chapter 3 — Band 8 and Band 9: the exact descriptors

These are the official descriptors — memorise the *difference*.

| Criterion | **Band 8** | **Band 9** |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluency & Coherence** | Fluent with only very occasional repetition or self-correction. Hesitation may occasionally be used to find words or grammar, but most will be content-related. Topic development coherent, appropriate and relevant. | Fluent with only very occasional repetition or self-correction. Any hesitation is used **only** to prepare the content of the next utterance, **not** to find words or grammar. Speech situationally appropriate; cohesive features fully acceptable. Topic development fully coherent and appropriately extended. |
| **Lexical Resource** | Wide resource, readily and flexibly used to discuss all topics and convey precise meaning. Skilful use of less common and idiomatic items despite occasional inaccuracies in word choice and collocation. Effective use of paraphrase. | Total flexibility and precise use in all contexts. Sustained use of accurate and idiomatic language. |
| **Grammar** | Wide range of structures, flexibly used. Majority of sentences error-free. Occasional inappropriacies and non-systematic errors; a few basic errors may persist. | Structures precise and accurate at all times, apart from 'mistakes' characteristic of native speaker speech. |
| **Pronunciation** | Wide range of phonological features to convey precise/subtle meaning. Can sustain appropriate rhythm. Flexible stress and intonation across long utterances, despite occasional lapses. Easily understood; accent minimal effect. | Full range of phonological features. Flexible connected speech sustained throughout. **Effortlessly understood**. Accent has no effect on intelligibility. |

**The key jump:** Band 8 = fluent, mostly accurate, easily understood. Band 9 = hesitation only
for content, sustained idiomatic precision, structures accurate at all times, effortless
understanding. It is a jump in *consistency*, not in adding bigger words.

# PART 2 — THE FOUR CRITERIA

## Chapter 4 — Fluency and Coherence (25%)

**What it measures:** continuity, rate, effort; logical sequencing of spoken sentences; clear
marking of stages; relevance; cohesive devices.

**Band 8 requires:** long turns without noticeable effort; only very occasional repetition and
self-correction; hesitation mostly for **content**, not word/grammar search; coherent, relevant
topic development.

**Band 9 adds:** hesitation *only* to prepare the next utterance; fully appropriate speech for
the situation; fully coherent and appropriately extended development.

**How to build it:**
- Speak in **spoken sentences** — complete thought units ending with a pause and falling intonation.
- Use spoken discourse markers to mark stages: `Well`, `To begin with`, `What I found
  interesting was`, `As for...`, `Looking back`.
- Extend every answer with a reason or example so the examiner has something to mark.
- If you lose the word, paraphrase immediately rather than pausing to search.

**What caps you at 7:** mid-sentence hesitation and repetition that show problems accessing
language; over-use of a few connectives (`and then`, `because`).

## Chapter 5 — Lexical Resource (25%)

**What it measures:** variety; precision; appropriacy (meaning, style, collocation, attitude);
and the ability to **paraphrase** around a vocabulary gap.

**Band 8 requires:** a wide, flexible resource across all topics; skilful less-common and
idiomatic items despite occasional inaccuracies in word choice/collocation; effective paraphrase.

**Band 9 adds:** total flexibility and precision in all contexts; **sustained** accurate idiomatic language.

**How to build it:**
- Learn vocabulary in **collocations and chunks**, not single words: `a long way from`,
  `to be honest`, `it depends on`, `a sense of achievement`, `to keep in touch`.
- Prepare 25–30 topics with 5–8 useful expressions each (see Appendix 2).
- Use less common items **naturally**: `quite handy`, `a bit of a nightmare`, `it grew on me`.
- When you don't know a word, explain it: "It's the thing you use for..." — this is rewarded.

**What caps you at 7:** inappropriacies and a visible struggle with style/collocation; reaching
for the same few words repeatedly.

**Warning:** big words used inaccurately are worse than simple words used precisely. Band 8
rewards *skilful* use, not impressive-sounding words.

## Chapter 6 — Grammatical Range and Accuracy (25%)

**What it measures:** length of spoken sentences; subordinate clauses; complex verb phrases;
pre/post-modification; range of structures; **error density and communicative effect**.

**Band 8 requires:** a wide range flexibly used; majority of sentences error-free; occasional
non-systematic errors; a few basic errors may persist.

**Band 9 adds:** structures precise and accurate at all times, apart from mistakes typical of
native speech.

**Structures that earn range (use only if accurate):**
- Relative clauses: *the place **that I go to** is...*
- Concessives: *Although it's crowded, I still prefer it.*
- Conditionals: *If I had more time, I'd go more often.*
- Complex verb phrases: *I've been meaning to...*, *It might have been...*
- Participle phrases: *Having tried both, I'd say...*
- Pre/post-modification: *a small park **beside the river***, *the kind of place **people go to unwind***

**What caps you at 7:** frequent errors in complex structures; a few basic errors that persist.
At Band 8 the majority of sentences must be error-free — accuracy before ambition.

## Chapter 7 — Pronunciation (25%)

**What it measures:** chunking; rhythm and stress-timing; linking/connected speech; emphatic
stress and intonation; word/phoneme clarity; effect of accent on intelligibility.

**Band 8:** wide range of phonological features; sustained rhythm; flexible stress and intonation
across long utterances despite occasional lapses; easily understood.

**Band 9:** full range; flexible connected speech sustained throughout; effortlessly understood;
accent no effect.

**Key point:** this is **not** about losing your accent. It is about control and intelligibility.

**How to build it:**
- **Chunk:** speak in thought groups, not word by word. Pause at the end of a spoken sentence.
- **Stress content words**; let function words reduce. English is stress-timed.
- **Link sounds:** `want to → wanna`, `going to → gonna`, `next day → nex' day` in natural speech.
- **Use intonation** for meaning: rising for unfinished thoughts, falling for finished ones;
  emphatic stress for contrast (`I said *Tuesday*, not Monday`).
- **Fix word stress:** learn stress with new words (`PHOtograph`, `phoTOGraphy`).

Record 60 seconds of your speaking weekly and check these four things.

# PART 3 — THE THREE PARTS

## Chapter 8 — Part 1: Introduction and interview

**Shape:** Answer → Reason/Detail → Example (2–4 sentences, 15–30 seconds).

**Model answer (annotated)**

**Question:** *Where is your hometown, and what do you like about it?*

> "I'm from Chittagong, which is a port city in the southeast of Bangladesh. What I like most
> about it is the mix of the river and the hills — you can be at the beach in the morning and
> in the hills by the afternoon, which is quite unusual. I suppose the thing I miss most when
> I'm away is the food, especially the fresh fish."

**Why it works:** direct answer first · one clear reason · a concrete example · hedging (`I
suppose`) · relative clause (`which is...`) · natural informal tone.

**Tactics:** prepare everyday topics; use contractions; give a reason; don't monologue.
If the examiner interrupts, stop and listen — it is normal and not a penalty.

**Traps:** one-word answers; rehearsed-sounding answers; over-formal language.

## Chapter 9 — Part 2: The long turn

**Preparation (1 minute):** write 3–4 keywords covering **all** bullet points + one example and
one personal detail. Never write a script.

**Talk structure:** Introduction → cover each bullet in order → conclusion/reflection.

**Extension methods:**
- **PREP:** Point → Reason → Example → Point again.
- **Past / present / future.**
- **Describe → explain → evaluate.**
- **Personal angle:** "What made it memorable for me was..."

**Model cue card**
> Describe a place where you like to relax. You should say: where it is, when you go there,
> what you do there, and explain why it helps you relax.

**1-minute notes:** park beside river · 10 min from centre · evenings/weekends · walk 20 min,
read, phone off · quiet, green, water · reset after work

**Model talk (Band 8–9, annotated)**

> "Well, the place I'd like to talk about is a small park not far from where I live, right
> beside the river. **To begin with**, I suppose I go there two or three times a week, usually
> in the evening after work, and a bit more often at weekends. **What makes it special** is
> that it's surprisingly quiet — **although** it's only ten minutes from the city centre, you
> can't hear the traffic once you're inside. **When I get there** I normally walk along the
> river for about twenty minutes, and then I sit on a bench with a book for a while. I always
> switch my phone off, which is probably the most important part of the routine.
>
> **As for why it helps me relax**, I'd say it's the combination of the green space and the
> sound of the water. There's something about watching the river that makes my mind slow
> down, and **by the time** I walk home I usually feel I can think clearly again. I remember
> one evening last month **when** I'd had a really difficult day at work — I must have been
> there for nearly an hour, and I came back feeling completely different. That's **when** I
> realised it's not just about the exercise; it's about having a break from constant decisions
> and notifications. **Of course**, it isn't always peaceful — at weekends there are families
> and dog walkers — but even then I find the atmosphere pleasant rather than stressful.
> **So** yes, that's the place I go to when I need to reset."

**Features to notice:** clear signposting (**To begin with, As for, Of course, So**) · hedging
(`I suppose`, `I'd say`, `probably`) · complex grammar (`although`, relative clauses, `by the
time`, past perfect) · specific detail and a personal story · precise lexis (`surprisingly
quiet`, `combination`, `constant decisions`, `reset`) · full 2-minute development covering every bullet.

**Traps:** ignoring a bullet; going off topic; memorising a story that doesn't fit; long silences.

## Chapter 10 — Part 3: Discussion

**Shape:** Position → Explanation → Example → Contrast/Nuance → Conclusion.

**Model question:** *Do you think technology changes the way people learn?*

**Model answer (annotated)**

> "I'd say yes, but it depends how it's used. **The main way** it changes learning is access —
> students can now find explanations, videos and practice material within seconds, which simply
> wasn't possible twenty years ago. **That said**, I think there's a downside: it's much easier
> to be distracted, and some students end up skimming rather than really thinking things
> through. **For example**, my cousin uses an app to revise, but he told me he often checks
> his messages at the same time, so I'm not convinced he's learning as much as he thinks.
> **In the long run**, I suspect the schools that do best will be the ones that teach students
> how to use technology deliberately, rather than just giving them devices."

**Features:** clear position with hedging (`I'd say`, `it depends`) · structured development
(`The main way`, `That said`, `For example`, `In the long run`) · contrast and nuance · relevant
example · complex structures (`which wasn't possible`, `the ones that`, `how to use`) ·
topic vocabulary (`access`, `skimming`, `deliberately`).

**Tactics:** give general views, not just personal stories; speculate; hedge; acknowledge the
other side. The examiner may challenge you — that is a chance to show nuance, not a criticism.

**Traps:** yes/no answers without development; repeating Part 2; treating a challenge as a
threat; over-personal answers to general questions.

# PART 4 — THE TOOLKIT

## Chapter 11 — Fillers, hesitation and self-correction

- Small fillers (`Well`, `I mean`, `Let me think`, `That's an interesting one`) are **natural**
  when used to plan **content**.
- At Band 9, hesitation is used **only** for content — never to search for words or grammar.
  This single distinction is the clearest 8→9 boundary in Fluency.
- Occasional self-correction is acceptable; frequent self-correction signals accessing problems.
- A short silent pause is better than a chain of `umm`, `er`, false starts and backtracking.

## Chapter 12 — Paraphrase: your emergency tool

When you don't know a word, keep speaking:
- "It's the thing you use to..."
- "I can't remember the word, but it's a bit like..."
- "What I mean is..."

Paraphrase is explicitly rewarded in Lexical Resource. Freezing to search for the perfect word
damages both LR and Fluency.

## Chapter 13 — Pronunciation drills

1. **Shadowing** — speak along with a short native clip, copying chunking, stress and intonation.
2. **Marked script** — mark `/` for pauses and underline stressed words in your own model answer, then read it aloud.
3. **Minimal pairs** — `ship/sheep`, `live/leave`, `thirty/thirteen`; record and compare.
4. **Weekly recording** — 60 seconds; check chunking, fillers, clarity.
5. **Word stress log** — learn stress with each new word.

## Chapter 14 — Memorisation and other traps

- **Never memorise answers.** Recognised memorised speech is penalised, and it collapses when
  the question changes slightly.
- **Examiner interruption is normal**, especially in Part 3 — stop and answer the new question.
- **Going off topic** reduces coherence — answer the question asked.
- **Not all examiners smile** — this is not a signal about your score.
- **Asking the examiner questions** is generally unnecessary and can waste time.
- **You may tell small stories** — the content doesn't need to be factually important; it just
  needs to demonstrate language.

## Chapter 15 — Topic bank preparation

Prepare **25–30 topics**, each with:
- 5–8 collocations/expressions
- one personal story reusable across several Part 2 prompts
- 2–3 Part 3 opinions with reasons and examples

Common areas: home, hometown, family, friends, work, study, hobbies, food, travel, transport,
technology, health, sleep, environment, education, culture, art, music, sport, money, weather,
colours, happiness, skills, memories, places, people.

# PART 5 — PRACTICE AND PROGRESS

## Chapter 16 — Self-assessment

Record a full 3-part mock. Score each criterion 1–9 using the descriptors in Chapter 3.
Be strict:
- Did any hesitation occur because you searched for a word or grammar? → FC below 9.
- Were the majority of sentences error-free? → GRA 8+.
- Did you use idiomatic language *accurately* and *sustainably*? → LR 8+.
- Could you be understood **effortlessly**, without any accent effect? → Pron 9.

Average the four criteria across all three parts for your predicted band.

## Chapter 17 — Mock test routine

1. Record the whole test, with proper timing (1 min prep in Part 2).
2. Transcribe one answer verbatim — you cannot judge Fluency or Pronunciation from memory.
3. Grade each criterion with evidence from the transcript.
4. Pick the single lowest criterion and set one drill for the week.
5. Re-record the same answer after a week and compare.

## Chapter 18 — Four-week plan to Band 8–9

- **Week 1:** Part 1 — 10 topics, daily 2-minute recordings; focus on Answer→Reason→Example.
- **Week 2:** Part 2 — 10 cue cards, strict 1-min prep and 2-min talk; focus on covering all bullets.
- **Week 3:** Part 3 — 10 abstract questions; focus on position→explanation→example→nuance.
- **Week 4:** 3 full mocks, transcribed and graded; fix the weakest criterion; pronunciation drills daily.

## Chapter 19 — From Band 8 to Band 9

| | **Band 8** | **Band 9** |
|---|---|---|
| Hesitation | Mostly content-related; occasionally word/grammar search | **Only** content-planning |
| Language | Wide, skilful, occasional inappropriacy | Precise and idiomatic throughout |
| Grammar | Majority error-free; a few basic errors persist | Precise and accurate at all times |
| Pronunciation | Easily understood; occasional lapses | Effortlessly understood; sustained connected speech |
| Development | Coherent, relevant | Fully coherent, appropriately extended |

**The five moves:**
1. **Make hesitation content-only.** Have a paraphrase ready instead of searching.
2. **Sustain idiomatic precision** across the whole test, not just in one good answer.
3. **Eliminate persistent basic errors** — they are what keep Band 8 from becoming 9.
4. **Control connected speech** across long turns, not just in short answers.
5. **Extend appropriately** — fully develop ideas without rambling or repeating.

Remember: Band 9 is not bigger words. It is effortless, accurate, natural consistency.

# APPENDIX 1 — QUICK REFERENCE CARD

**Part 1:** Answer → Reason → Example. 2–4 sentences. Contractions fine.
**Part 2:** 1 min notes (keywords only) → intro → bullets in order → reflection. Aim 2 min.
**Part 3:** Position → Explanation → Example → Contrast → Conclusion. Hedge and nuance.

**Four criteria (25% each):** Fluency & Coherence · Lexical Resource · Grammar · Pronunciation.
**Band 8:** occasional repetition, mostly content hesitation, majority error-free, easily understood.
**Band 9:** content-only hesitation, sustained idiomatic precision, precise/accurate structures, effortless understanding.

**Never memorise. Never freeze — paraphrase. Interruption is normal.**
