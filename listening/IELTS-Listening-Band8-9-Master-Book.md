# IELTS Listening — Band 8–9 Master Book

**Every question type. How to face it. Tricks, tips and traps.**

> Target: Band 8–9. Based on the official IELTS Listening format and scoring, the official
> question-type list, and established IELTS teaching practice. Verified November 2026.
> There is **one Listening test for both Academic and General Training** candidates.

---

## How to use this book

1. **Part 1** — the rules. Memorise the answer-form rules; they are free marks.
2. **Part 2** — the core skills. Prediction is the single most important habit.
3. **Part 3** — one chapter per question type: what it asks, how to face it, tricks, traps, examples.
4. **Part 4** — the hard parts (Sections 3–4, self-corrections, maps).
5. **Part 5** — exam-day and timing plan.
6. **Part 6** — error analysis and a full worked practice set with transcript and answer key.
7. Keep a **paraphrase + spelling log** from every practice test.

# PART 1 — KNOW THE TEST

## Chapter 1 — Format

- **4 parts, 10 questions each = 40 questions.**
- **Recording ≈ 30 minutes; total test ≈ 40 minutes** including transfer/checking time.
- **You hear everything once only.** No pause, no replay.
- **Questions follow the order of the recording** — answer 1 comes before answer 2, always.
- **Different accents:** British, Australian, New Zealand and North American, among others.
- **One test for everyone** — Academic and GT candidates sit the same paper with the same scoring.

| Part | Speakers | Context |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two speakers | Everyday/social situation, e.g. travel arrangements |
| 2 | One main speaker | Everyday/social, e.g. a talk about local facilities |
| 3 | Two (sometimes three) speakers | Educational/training, e.g. students with a tutor |
| 4 | One speaker | Academic subject, e.g. a lecture |

## Chapter 2 — Scoring and bands

Each correct answer = 1 mark. No negative marking.

IELTS publishes **average** marks needed at each band; exact cut-offs vary slightly:

| Band | Average correct out of 40 |
|---|---|
| 5 | 16 |
| 6 | 23 |
| 7 | 30 |
| 8 | 35 |
| 9 | ~39–40 (approximate) |

For **Band 8–9**, aim for 35+ correct, with 38–40 the realistic Band 9 zone. One careless
plural or spelling error is the difference between 8 and 9.

## Chapter 3 — Transfer and checking time

| Mode | During recording | After recording |
|---|---|---|
| **Paper** | Write answers on the question paper | **10 minutes** to transfer to the answer sheet — only the answer sheet is marked |
| **Computer** | Type answers directly into the computer | **2 minutes** to check; then the screen locks |

- The test itself is identical — only answer entry differs.
- From mid-2026 IELTS is computer-delivered in most markets, so expect **2 minutes** to check.
- Use that time for spelling, plurals and word limits — not for re-thinking answers.
- There is **no example answer** at the start of the recording in the current format.

## Chapter 4 — Answer-form rules (memorise)

- **Instructions are set per question type and can change within a part.** Read them every time.
- **Word limits:** "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER". Three words when the limit is two is wrong. A number alone is fine.
- **If asked for a letter, write a letter** (A, B, C) — not the whole word.
- **Spelling and grammar count.** Singular for plural = wrong.
- **Capitalisation does not matter.** British and American spellings are both accepted.
- **Numbers:** digits or words both accepted (`20` / `twenty`); dates `21 March` or `21st March`.
- **Brackets in an answer key mean optional words.**
- **No negative marking** — never leave a blank.
- Answers can be changed during the test, but not after transfer/checking ends.

## Chapter 5 — The question types at a glance

Six official types; ten practical types after splitting completion formats:

1. Form / note / table / flow-chart completion
2. Sentence completion
3. Summary completion
4. Short-answer questions
5. Multiple choice
6. Matching
7. List selection
8. Plan / map / diagram labelling
9. Matching sentence endings
10. Classification / categorization

**All answers follow the order of the recording.** Use this as your compass.

# PART 2 — THE CORE SKILLS

## Chapter 6 — Prediction

Before each recording (you are given reading time):

1. Read the instructions — word limit and answer format.
2. Underline keywords in each question.
3. Predict the **word type**: noun, number, date, adjective, name.
4. Predict the **kind of meaning**: a place, a problem, a reason, a person.
5. If possible, predict a likely word — but stay open-minded.

Example: gap = "The meeting was moved to ______." After "to" you expect a time or day.
Now you listen for a day, not for a name.

Prediction turns passive listening into **targeted** listening.

## Chapter 7 — Keywords and paraphrase

The recording rarely repeats the question wording:
`job → position` · `cheap → inexpensive` · `problem → issue` · `children → young learners`.

- Listen for **meaning**, not identical words.
- Keep your eyes on the *next* question as you listen to the current one — this is the
  multi-tasking skill.
- If you miss an answer: **guess, write something, move on immediately.** One missed answer
  must not cost you the next three. The order rule lets you recover.

## Chapter 8 — Plurals, numbers and spelling

These are the silent band killers.

- **Plurals:** listen for the final `s`. "We need three chair**s**" → answer: chairs. A
  singular answer is wrong.
- **Numbers:** practise `13/30`, `14/40`, `15/50`, `16/60`, prices, dates and phone numbers.
  Write digits to save time.
- **Names:** speakers often spell them out letter by letter (`J-E-N-S-E-N`). Write carefully.
- **Addresses:** listen for the street name and number; check spelling in the transfer time.

Daily drills: 10 numbers, 5 spelled names, 5 plural sentences.

## Chapter 9 — Section-by-section strategy

- **Part 1 — social conversation.** Easiest. Names, dates, numbers, addresses, spelling.
  Answers are usually direct. Check plurals.
- **Part 2 — single speaker, social.** Often a map/tour or facilities talk. Track location
  and direction; watch for changes to the original plan.
- **Part 3 — educational conversation.** Track **who** holds which view. Watch for disagreement
  and changing opinions ("I used to think X, but now...").
- **Part 4 — academic monologue.** Hardest. Note/summary completion. No conversational help —
  rely on prediction and signpost words (`firstly`, `however`, `in conclusion`).

## Chapter 10 — The 7-step method for every question

1. Read instructions — word limit, letter vs word.
2. Underline keywords.
3. Predict word type and likely meaning.
4. Listen for the keyword **or paraphrase**.
5. Watch for self-corrections and distractors.
6. Write the exact answer within the limit.
7. Check plural, spelling and form during transfer/check time.

# PART 3 — THE QUESTION-TYPE PLAYBOOK

## Chapter 11 — Form / note / table / flow-chart completion

**What it asks:** fill gaps in a structured answer sheet.

**How to face it:**
1. Read the instructions and the word limit.
2. Identify what each gap needs — noun, number, date, adjective.
3. Underline keywords around each gap.
4. Listen for the keyword or paraphrase.
5. Copy the exact word or number from the recording.
6. Check plural and spelling.

**Tricks & tips:** forms often spell out names and addresses. Table headings tell you the
answer type. In a flow chart, follow the arrows — the order matches the recording.

**Traps:** exceeding the word limit; wrong plural; confusing `13` and `30`.

**Mini example**
> Transcript: "The appointment is on the twelfth of June — sorry, the nineteenth."
> Question: Date: ______
> **Answer: 19 June.** The self-correction replaces the first date.

## Chapter 12 — Sentence completion

**What it asks:** complete a sentence with words/numbers from the recording.

**How to face it:** predict from the grammar; underline keywords; listen for paraphrase;
copy the exact word; re-read the completed sentence.

**Trick:** grammar predicts the answer — after "the" expect a noun; after "was" an adjective
or past participle.

**Trap:** writing a synonym instead of the word actually used.

## Chapter 13 — Summary completion

**What it asks:** fill gaps in a short summary of the recording.

**How to face it:** read the whole summary first; predict word types; use the order rule
(the summary follows the recording); copy exact words.

**Trick:** because the summary mirrors the recording's order, you always know roughly where
you are.

**Trap:** a word that fits the meaning but was not said.

## Chapter 14 — Short-answer questions

**What it asks:** answer with a short phrase from the recording.

**How to face it:** underline question keywords; predict answer type; listen for paraphrase;
write the exact short phrase within the limit.

**Trick:** answers are usually noun phrases, not full sentences.

**Trap:** extra words that push you over the limit.

## Chapter 15 — Multiple choice

**What it asks:** choose the correct option (A/B/C) for a question or sentence ending.

**How to face it:** read the stem and ALL options before the recording; underline the
differences between options; predict; listen for the paraphrase of the right option;
eliminate distractors.

**Tricks & tips:** wrong options often repeat words from the recording but attach them to
the wrong speaker, time or reason. Listen for `actually`, `well, no`, `sorry`, `I mean`,
`on second thoughts` — the corrected information is the answer. Watch for "NOT"/"EXCEPT"
in the stem.

**Trap:** choosing the first option you hear mentioned.

## Chapter 16 — Matching

**What it asks:** match items (people, places, opinions) to a list of options.

**How to face it:** read the list and the items; predict; listen for names and opinions;
answers follow order.

**Trick:** there are usually **more options than questions** — use each once unless told otherwise.

**Trap:** matching a view to the person who *mentioned* it rather than the person who *holds* it.

## Chapter 17 — List selection

**What it asks:** choose which items from a list are required or mentioned.

**How to face it:** read the whole list; listen for each item; tick as you go.

**Trick:** a distractor may be mentioned but rejected ("we considered X but decided against it").

**Trap:** ticking an item merely because it was mentioned.

## Chapter 18 — Plan / map / diagram labelling

**What it asks:** label a map, floor plan, diagram or process picture.

**How to face it:**
1. Study the picture *before* the recording — note orientation, entrance, landmarks and arrow direction.
2. Identify what each label needs.
3. Listen for directions (`turn left`, `opposite`, `next to`, `on your right`) and trace the route with your finger.
4. Write exact words within the limit.

**Tricks & tips:** directions are from the speaker's/listener's viewpoint — check which way
they are facing. Landmarks are more reliable than distances. In process diagrams, follow arrow order.

**Traps:** confusing left and right; losing orientation after one wrong turn; misspelling labels.

## Chapter 19 — Matching sentence endings

**What it asks:** choose the correct ending for each sentence beginning.

**How to face it:** read all beginnings and endings; predict; listen for the meaning; check
the completed sentence is grammatical.

**Trick:** grammar eliminates endings that do not fit.

**Trap:** there are usually more endings than beginnings.

## Chapter 20 — Classification / categorization

**What it asks:** decide which category a statement belongs to (e.g. which person holds which opinion).

**How to face it:** read categories and statements; listen for attribution and reporting verbs;
match the meaning.

**Trick:** "I used to think X, but now I think Y" — the **final** position is the answer.

**Trap:** assigning an opinion to the wrong speaker.

# PART 4 — THE HARD ONES

## Chapter 21 — Distractors and self-corrections

The recording is designed to mislead. Common signals:

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `actually`, `well, no`, `sorry`, `I mean`, `on second thoughts` | The speaker is correcting themselves — the NEW information is the answer |
| `but`, `however`, `although`, `in fact`, `instead` | A contrast — the information after the signal often overrides what came before |
| `we planned to... but...` | The original plan is a distractor; the changed plan is the answer |

**Rule:** if you hear a number or name and then a correction, write the correction.

## Chapter 22 — Surviving Sections 3 and 4

- **Section 3:** focus on **who** says what. Note names next to opinions. Watch for one
  speaker changing their mind. Don't assume the "expert" is always right — you only need
  to report what is said.
- **Section 4:** no conversation, no pauses for effect. Use signpost words to segment the
  lecture: `firstly`, `then`, `another factor`, `by contrast`, `in conclusion`.
  Prediction from the notes is essential. Write words in the gaps as you hear them.

## Chapter 23 — Map questions in detail

1. Locate **You are here** and the entrance.
2. Note the compass or landmark references before listening.
3. Follow the speaker's route physically with your finger or pen.
4. Convert "on your left" carefully: whose left? The speaker's direction of travel.
5. Check the answer's spelling in the transfer time.

# PART 5 — TIMING AND EXAM DAY

## Chapter 24 — Test-day plan

- Use the reading time before each part to predict every answer.
- Keep your eyes one question ahead of your ears.
- If you miss one, guess and move on — never chase a lost answer.
- At the end:
  - **Computer:** use the 2 minutes to check spelling, plurals and word limits.
  - **Paper:** use all 10 minutes to transfer carefully, then check spelling and plurals.
- Never leave a blank.

## Chapter 25 — Exam-day checklist

- [ ] Read instructions for each question set — word limit, letter vs word.
- [ ] Predict word type for every gap.
- [ ] Underline keywords.
- [ ] Watch for self-corrections.
- [ ] Check plurals, numbers and spelling.
- [ ] Guess every unanswered item.

# PART 6 — PRACTICE AND ERROR ANALYSIS

## Chapter 26 — Error taxonomy

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| Lost place | Guess and move on; use the order rule |
| Missed paraphrase | Build paraphrase lists from transcripts |
| Plural/spelling error | Listen for final `s`; daily spelling drills |
| Number error | 13/30 drills; write digits |
| Word-limit breach | Circle the limit before listening |
| Distractor trap | Listen for self-corrections |
| Prediction failure | Predict every gap before listening |
| Concentration lapse | Daily focused practice |
| Transfer/copy error | Use transfer time carefully |

## Chapter 27 — Analyse, don't accumulate

1. Do a section under exam conditions (one play only).
2. Check answers **with the transcript** — quote the exact evidence line.
3. Mark the paraphrase (question wording → transcript wording).
4. Classify each error.
5. Replay and dictate the difficult sentences.
6. Keep a personal list of problem numbers, names and spellings.

## Chapter 28 — Full worked practice set

**Instructions:** Complete the notes below. Write **NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER**
for each answer. For question 8, write the correct letter, A, B or C.

### Transcript

> **Receptionist:** Good morning, Riverside Leisure Centre. How can I help?
> **Caller:** Hi, I'd like to book a court for badminton.
> **Receptionist:** Certainly. Could I take your name?
> **Caller:** It's Sarah Jensen. That's J-E-N-S-E-N.
> **Receptionist:** Thank you, Sarah. And which day were you thinking of?
> **Caller:** Saturday afternoon, if possible.
> **Receptionist:** Saturday the twelfth? I'm afraid we're fully booked then. We do have space on Sunday the thirteenth.
> **Caller:** Sunday works, actually. What time is available?
> **Receptionist:** We've got a slot at two thirty, or at four. The four o'clock slot is cheaper — it's six pounds rather than eight.
> **Caller:** Let's take the four o'clock one then.
> **Receptionist:** Right. Now, do you need to hire equipment? A racket is three pounds fifty, or you can bring your own.
> **Caller:** I'll bring my own, thanks. But I do need a shuttlecock — actually, no, I've got one.
> **Receptionist:** Fine. Oh, we're currently offering a free towel with every booking, but only on weekdays.
> **Caller:** Not needed, thanks. What's the address again? I'll be driving.
> **Receptionist:** We're on Mill Lane, opposite the library. The entrance is on the left as you come from the town centre. There's parking behind the building.
> **Caller:** Great. And do I need to bring anything else?
> **Receptionist:** Just your membership card. Oh — the reception closes at five, so please arrive before then.

### Questions

1. Name: Sarah ______
2. Date of booking: ______
3. Time of booking: ______
4. Cost of chosen slot: £______
5. Which day did the caller FIRST ask for?
   A. Saturday 12th · B. Sunday 13th · C. Friday 11th
6. The caller will bring her own ______.
7. The centre is opposite the ______.
8. Which item is free with weekday bookings?
   A. A towel · B. A racket · C. A shuttlecock
9. The entrance is on the ______ as you come from the town centre.
10. The caller must bring her ______ ______.

### Answer key with transcript evidence

1. **Jensen** — "It's Sarah Jensen. That's J-E-N-S-E-N." (spelling drill)
2. **13 / 13th / Sunday 13th** — "We do have space on Sunday the thirteenth." (the 12th is a distractor)
3. **4 / 4 pm / four** — "Let's take the four o'clock one then." (2:30 was rejected)
4. **6 / six** — "it's six pounds rather than eight" (the cheaper slot was chosen)
5. **A** — "Saturday afternoon, if possible." → the first request was Saturday 12th
6. **racket** — "A racket is three pounds fifty, or you can bring your own" → "I'll bring my own, thanks."
7. **library** — "We're on Mill Lane, opposite the library."
8. **A** — "a free towel with every booking, but only on weekdays" (trap: the booking is Sunday, so no free towel; the question asks which item is offered on weekdays)
9. **left** — "The entrance is on the left as you come from the town centre."
10. **membership card** — "Just your membership card."

### Paraphrase / trap map

| Question wording | Transcript wording | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| "Date of booking" | "Sunday the thirteenth" | The 12th was mentioned first but rejected |
| "Cost of chosen slot" | "six pounds rather than eight" | Must track which slot was chosen |
| "bring her own" | "or you can bring your own" | Reference must be resolved to "racket" |
| "opposite" | "opposite the library" | Answer after the keyword |
| "free with weekday bookings" | "free towel... only on weekdays" | Booking is Sunday — don't assume the offer applies |

## Chapter 29 — From Band 8 to Band 9

| | **Band 8** | **Band 9** |
|---|---|---|
| Raw score (approx.) | ~35/40 | ~39–40/40 |
| Careless errors | A few tolerated | Almost none |
| Plurals/spelling | Usually right | Always right |
| Following order | Mostly maintained | Never lost |
| Paraphrase | Recognises common ones | Instantly recognises complex ones |

**What separates 8 from 9:**
1. **Zero avoidable errors** — plural `s`, spelling of names, word limits, numbers.
2. **Never lose your place** — one miss must never cascade. Guess and move on.
3. **Instant paraphrase recognition** — build the log from every transcript.
4. **Master accents** — expose yourself to British, Australian, NZ and North American speech daily.
5. **Use transfer/check time perfectly** — check every answer for form, not meaning.
6. **Benchmark:** consistently 38+/40 on timed authentic Cambridge tests before expecting 9.
