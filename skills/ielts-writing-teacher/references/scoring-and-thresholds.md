# IELTS Writing: Scoring, Thresholds and Current Rules

Source base: official IELTS partners (ielts.org, IDP IELTS), the official public
*Writing Band Descriptors* (Updated May 2023) and *Writing Key Assessment Criteria*
PDFs, and the transcripts of the official examiner videos. Verified November 2026.

## 1. The scale

- Writing is reported on the **IELTS 9-band scale in whole and half bands** only.
- 9 Expert · 8 Very good · 7 Good · 6 Competent · 5 Modest · 4 Limited ·
  3 Extremely limited · 2 Intermittent · 1 Non-user · 0 Did not attempt.
- Band descriptors are **criterion-referenced**. A script "must fully fit the positive
  features of the descriptor at a particular level"; bolded negative features limit a
  rating. There is no fixed number of errors that equals a band.

## 2. The scoring pipeline

1. **Four criteria, equally weighted (25% each) per task:**
   - Task 1: Task Achievement (TA), Coherence & Cohesion (CC), Lexical Resource (LR),
     Grammatical Range & Accuracy (GRA).
   - Task 2: Task Response (TR) replaces Task Achievement; other three are identical.
2. **Task band** = mean of the four criterion bands.
3. **Task weighting:** "Each task is assessed independently. The criteria are weighted
   equally and the score on the task is the average. The assessment of Task 2 carries
   more weight in marking than Task 1." The official examiner video says explicitly:
   *"Task 1 is worth a third of your overall mark for Writing. Task 2 is worth two thirds."*
   `Writing band = (Task 1 + 2 × Task 2) ÷ 3`
4. **Rounding:** IELTS publicly documents the rule for the **Overall Band Score**:
   average ending in `.25` → round up to the next half band; ending in `.75` → round up
   to the next whole band. IELTS does **not** publish a separate explicit rounding rule
   for the Writing component; standard practice is round to the nearest half band.
   Examples: (6 + 14)/3 = 6.67 → ~6.5; (7 + 12)/3 = 6.33 → ~6.5; (6.5 + 14)/3 = 6.83 → ~7.0.
5. **Overall band** = (Listening + Reading + Writing + Speaking) ÷ 4, rounded as above.

## 3. What each criterion measures

- **TA (T1):** how fully, appropriately, accurately and relevantly the response fulfils
  the task (min 150 words). Academic: select key features, support with figures/data,
  compare/contrast, present an **overview**, appropriate format. GT: explain purpose,
  cover all **3 bullet points**, extend them, correct letter format, consistent tone.
- **TR (T2):** how fully all parts of the prompt are addressed, how main ideas are
  extended/supported with relevant evidence (min 250 words), clarity of position,
  opening/conclusion, appropriate format.
- **CC:** logical organisation and progression, paragraphing, sequencing within
  paragraphs, reference/substitution, discourse markers — flexible and appropriate,
  not mechanical or overused.
- **LR:** range, precision, appropriacy and accuracy of vocabulary; collocations and
  less common/idiomatic items; spelling and word-formation errors and their impact.
- **GRA:** range of simple/compound/complex structures and complex features (passives,
  modals, comparatives, noun phrases), sentence accuracy, error density and effect,
  punctuation.

## 4. Hidden thresholds from the official examiner videos

These are the practical cut-offs examiners use and are the most useful diagnostic tool:

- **Academic T1 overview:** clear overview needed for **Band 7+**; "relevant overview
  attempted" = **Band 6**; no real overview → **max Band 5**. An introduction is not an
  overview.
- **GT T1:** appropriate letter format for **Band 6+**; clear purpose = **Band 7+**;
  all three bullet points covered = **Band 6+**; extend bullet functions (e.g. plural
  prompts need more than one suggestion).
- **T2 prompt coverage:** address **all** main parts for **Band 6+**. Miss one part
  (e.g. discuss effects on children but not families) → **max Band 5**. Clear, developed
  position = **Band 7+**.
- **Coherence:** in T1 paragraphing only really matters for **Bands 8–9**; in T2
  paragraphs are expected at **Band 6+** and used effectively at 7+, with a central topic
  per paragraph and logical sequencing inside it.
- **LR:** "minimally adequate" range = **Band 5**; spelling/word-formation errors that
  cause reader difficulty → **Band 5 cap**; "awareness of style and collocation" with
  occasional inappropriacies = **Band 7**; flexible precise use = 8–9.
- **GRA:** range of sentence forms needed for **Band 6+**; complex structures for **7+**;
  majority error-free with only **non-systematic** errors = **Band 8** (repeated
  systematic errors, e.g. articles, prevent 8); frequent errors causing difficulty →
  **Band 5 cap**.
- **Band 8 vs 7:** for Band 8 errors must be non-systematic and the majority of
  sentences error-free. That distinction is not required at Band 7.
- **Band 9/8 profile:** message followed effortlessly/with ease; cohesion rarely
  attracts attention; errors extremely rare with minimal impact; skilful paragraphing.

## 5. Penalties, ceilings and special cases

- **≤ 20 words** in a response → **Band 1** across criteria.
- **Notes, bullet points, sub-headings, copied rubric or diagrams** → penalised; answers
  must be full connected text. Note-taking tool notes are not seen by the examiner.
- **Plagiarism / wholly memorised answers** → severely penalised; proof of totally
  memorised answer, use of another language throughout, or not attempting → **Band 0**.
- **Off-topic / largely irrelevant** → **Band 2–3**.
- **Underlength** (under 150 / 250): no fixed deduction, but too little evidence of
  higher-band features → limits the band.
- **Overlength:** no penalty, but it eats time (fatal when T2 is worth double) and can
  introduce irrelevant material.
- There is **no fixed "X mistakes = Band Y" rule** — frequency and communicative effect
  determine the band.

## 6. Who marks it, and quality control

- Certificated IELTS examiners: relevant teaching qualification and extensive teaching
  experience; performance subject to regular detailed review (IDP).
- IDP states a Writing test is marked by **between 2 and 4 examiners** for accuracy and
  fairness.
- IELTS is **regulated by Ofqual** worldwide. In June 2026 IELTS disclosed an Ofqual
  enforcement action over a marking incident (24 Aug 2023 – 4 Sep 2025) affecting
  63,216 test instances, mostly 0.5-band corrections; it says it added operational
  controls. Context for score-reliability questions.
- IELTS's May 2026 article on **automarking**: AI can assist/flag responses but a hybrid
  model with human oversight is essential and AI cannot be the sole determinant of a
  high-stakes result. Writing is currently human-marked.

## 7. Current (2026) test rules

- **From mid-2026:** IELTS is phasing out paper-based testing — all IELTS delivered on
  **computer** (exact timing varies by market). Selected markets offer **Writing on
  Paper**: prompts on screen, handwritten answers on an answer sheet. Same tasks,
  scoring and 60-minute timing; Academic and GT only, **not UKVI**; results within
  5 days; research found scores comparable across formats; skills assessed and score
  interpretation unchanged.
- **One Skill Retake** covers Writing in participating countries; for Writing on Paper
  the retake must be in the same mode.
- **From 15 July 2026:** TRFs carry the Ofqual logo and qualification title — a design
  change only; older TRFs remain valid.
- **Validity:** IELTS recommends results are considered valid for **2 years**.
