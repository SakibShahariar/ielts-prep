# IELTS Listening: Format, Scoring and Rules

Source base: official IELTS partners (ielts.org), IELTS Liz, and other IELTS
preparation references. Verified November 2026.

## 1. Format (identical for Academic and General Training)

- **One test for all candidates.** Academic and GT candidates take the same Listening test with the same scoring.
- **4 parts, 10 questions each = 40 questions.**
- **Recording lasts about 30 minutes; total test time about 40 minutes** (including transfer/checking time).
- **You hear each recording once only.** No pause, no replay.
- **Questions follow the order of information** in the recording: answer 1 comes before answer 2.
- **Accents:** British, Australian, New Zealand and North American, among others.
- **Each correct answer = 1 mark.** No negative marking.

| Part | Speakers | Context |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conversation between two speakers | Everyday/social situation, e.g. travel arrangements |
| 2 | One main speaker | Everyday/social, e.g. a speech about local facilities |
| 3 | Conversation between two (sometimes three) speakers | Educational/training, e.g. students discussing work with a tutor |
| 4 | One speaker | Academic subject, e.g. a lecture |

## 2. Official question types (six)

1. Multiple choice
2. Matching
3. Plan / map / diagram labelling
4. Form / note / table / flow-chart completion
5. Sentence completion
6. Short-answer questions

Practitioners often count ten practical types (see `question-types.md`), splitting the
completion formats and adding list selection.

## 3. Transfer / checking time

- **Paper-based:** write answers on the question paper during the recording; at the end you get **10 minutes** to transfer to the answer sheet. Only the answer sheet is marked. When time ends you cannot write another word.
- **Computer-based:** type answers directly into the computer during the test; at the end you get **2 minutes** to check. The screen then locks.
- This is the only difference between paper and computer Listening — the test itself is the same.
- From mid-2026 IELTS is computer-delivered only in most markets (with "Writing on Paper" available in selected markets for Writing only).

## 4. Raw score → band

IELTS publishes **average** marks needed at each band. Exact cut-offs vary slightly by test version.

| Band | Average correct out of 40 |
|---|---|
| 5 | 16 |
| 6 | 23 |
| 7 | 30 |
| 8 | 35 |
| 9 | ~39–40 (approximate; not an officially published average here) |

For Band 8–9 aim for **35+ correct**, with 38–40 the realistic Band 9 zone.

## 5. Answer-form rules

- **Instructions are set per question type and can change within a part.** Read them every time.
- **Word limits:** e.g. "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER". Three words when the limit is two is wrong; one number alone is fine.
- **If asked for a letter, write a letter** — not the whole word.
- **Spelling and grammar count.** A wrong plural or misspelling loses the mark.
- **Capitalisation does not matter.** British and American spellings are both accepted.
- **Numbers:** digits or words both accepted (`20` or `twenty`); dates may be written `21 March` or `21st March`.
- **Words in brackets in an answer key are optional.**
- **No penalty for wrong answers** — always guess rather than leave a blank.
- Answers can be changed during the test, but not after the transfer/checking time ends.
- There is no example answer at the start of the recording in the current test format.
