# IELTS Listening: Question Types (10 practical types)

All answers follow the order of the recording. Instructions (word limits, letter vs word)
are set **per question set** and can change within a part.

## 1. Form / note / table / flow-chart completion
- **Task:** fill gaps in a form, notes, a table or a flow chart.
- **How:** read the instructions and word limit; predict the word type for each gap (noun, number, date, adjective); underline keywords; listen for the keyword or paraphrase; write the exact word/number heard; check plural and spelling.
- **Tricks:** forms often contain names, addresses, dates and numbers — these are spelled out or repeated. Note the column/row headings in a table: they tell you what kind of answer is needed. In a flow chart, follow the arrows.
- **Traps:** extra words beyond the limit; wrong plural; confusing similar numbers (`thirteen` vs `thirty`).

## 2. Sentence completion
- **Task:** complete a sentence with words/numbers from the recording.
- **How:** predict the missing word type from the grammar of the sentence; underline keywords; listen for paraphrase; copy the exact word; re-read the completed sentence to check it makes sense.
- **Trick:** grammar predicts the answer — a gap after "the" is a noun; after "was" it may be an adjective or past participle.
- **Traps:** writing a synonym instead of the word used; ignoring the word limit.

## 3. Summary completion
- **Task:** fill gaps in a short summary of what you hear.
- **How:** read the whole summary first for context; predict word types; listen for the summary's order matching the recording's order; copy exact words.
- **Trick:** the summary usually follows the recording closely, so you can use order to know where you are.
- **Traps:** a word that fits the meaning but not the recording; plural errors.

## 4. Short-answer questions
- **Task:** answer questions with short phrases, usually from the recording.
- **How:** underline question keywords; predict the answer type; listen for paraphrase; write the exact short phrase within the limit.
- **Trick:** answers are usually noun phrases, not full sentences.
- **Trap:** over-answering — extra words can exceed the limit.

## 5. Multiple choice
- **Task:** choose the correct option (usually A/B/C) for a question or sentence ending.
- **How:** read the stem and all options before the recording; underline differences between options; predict; listen for the paraphrase of the correct option; eliminate distractors.
- **Tricks:** wrong options often repeat words from the recording but attach them to the wrong speaker, time or reason. Listen for self-corrections (`actually`, `well, no`, `sorry`) — the corrected version is the answer.
- **Traps:** choosing the first option mentioned; ignoring "NOT"/"EXCEPT" in the stem.

## 6. Matching
- **Task:** match items (people, places, opinions, features) to options from a list.
- **How:** read the list and question items; predict; listen for names/opinions and paraphrases; answers follow order.
- **Trick:** there are usually **more options than questions** — use each once unless told otherwise.
- **Trap:** matching a name to the wrong opinion because both were mentioned close together.

## 7. List selection
- **Task:** choose which items from a list are mentioned/required (often A–G).
- **How:** read the whole list; listen for each item; tick as you go.
- **Trick:** distractor items may be mentioned but not selected (e.g. "we considered X but decided against it").
- **Trap:** ticking an item merely because it was mentioned.

## 8. Plan / map / diagram labelling
- **Task:** label a map, floor plan, diagram or process picture.
- **How:** study the picture before listening — note orientation, entrance, landmarks and the direction of any arrows; identify what each label needs; listen for directions (`turn left`, `opposite`, `next to`, `on your right`) and follow the speaker's movement with your finger; write exact words.
- **Tricks:** directions are given from the listener's/speaker's viewpoint — check which way they are facing. Landmarks ("opposite the library") are more reliable than distances. In process diagrams, follow arrow order.
- **Traps:** confusing left and right; losing orientation after one wrong turn; misspelling labels.

## 9. Matching sentence endings
- **Task:** choose the correct ending for each sentence beginning from a list.
- **How:** read all beginnings and endings; predict; listen for the meaning; check the completed sentence is grammatical.
- **Trick:** grammar can eliminate endings that do not fit.
- **Trap:** there are usually more endings than beginnings.

## 10. Classification / categorization
- **Task:** decide which category a statement belongs to (e.g. which speaker holds which view).
- **How:** read categories and statements; listen for attribution and reporting verbs; match meaning.
- **Trick:** watch for `I used to think..., but now...` — the final position is the answer.
- **Trap:** assigning a view to the person who mentioned it rather than the person who holds it.
