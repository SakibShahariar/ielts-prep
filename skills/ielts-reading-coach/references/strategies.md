# IELTS Reading: Strategies, Timing and Error Analysis

## 1. The three core skills

1. **Skimming** — reading for the general idea and layout of the passage. Do this in
   **2–3 minutes** before the questions. Do not try to understand the passage; just get
   a sense of topic and structure, underlining keywords as you go. Exception: matching
   headings questions appear *before* the passage, so read the headings first.
2. **Scanning** — moving your eyes quickly across the text to locate specific words or
   information. This is a mechanical skill, not comprehension.
3. **Analysis** — once located, read the sentence(s) containing the answer deeply enough
   to decide, especially for T/F/NG and multiple choice.

Your aim is **not** to understand everything. It is to locate answers and win marks.

## 2. Keyword and paraphrase method

1. Underline the **keywords** in the question: names, dates, numbers, places are easy;
   conceptual words are subtler. Highlight every keyword — missing one (e.g. "abroad")
   changes the answer.
2. Prepare **paraphrases** before scanning: `work → employment`, `changing → altering`,
   `children → young learners`. Paraphrase can be a completely different phrasing, not
   just a single synonym.
3. Scan for the keyword OR its paraphrase.
4. Match the **whole meaning** in the sentence and its context — not individual words.
   The same words can appear with a different meaning.

Build a personal **paraphrase log** from every practice passage: question wording →
passage wording. This is the single highest-value study habit for Reading.

## 3. Timing plan (60 minutes, 40 questions)

- **Passage 1:** ~17–18 minutes · **Passage 2:** ~20 minutes · **Passage 3:** ~20–22 minutes
- Leave ~3–5 minutes at the end to transfer/check answers.
- Skim each passage in 2–3 minutes before answering its questions.
- If stuck on one question for more than ~90 seconds, mark it and move on. Return later.
- All answers must be written before the hour ends — there is no extra transfer time.
- In the computer test, use the highlight tool and keep an eye on the on-screen clock.
- You may tackle passages in any order; choose your strongest first if that helps.

## 4. True / False / Not Given deep dive

- Analyse the statement **before** searching: what exactly is being claimed? Break it
  into parts and check **every** part.
- **True:** the whole meaning is present (often paraphrased).
- **False:** the passage states something that contradicts the statement — the passage
  shows what the statement should be. It is not just "an opposite word".
- **Not Given:** the whole meaning is absent. Some keywords may appear, but if the full
  claim is not supported, it is NG.
- Answers follow passage order.
- Consider the **context of the whole passage**, not just one sentence.
- Watch for grammar that changes meaning: a passage saying "most working-class people
  used it for functional purposes, middle class for tourism" does NOT say "more working
  class people used it".
- Don't impose outside knowledge — answer only according to the passage.
- There is no rule that each of T, F and NG must appear, though sets of 4–6 questions
  commonly include all three.
- Grammar points that flip meaning: time (`by the second half of the 17th century`
  ≠ `after the 17th century`), quantifiers (`most`, `all`, `some`), comparatives,
  modality (`plans to` ≠ `currently does`).

## 5. Error taxonomy (use this when reviewing answers)

For every wrong answer, classify the cause:

| Cause | Signature | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary/paraphrase gap | Didn't recognise question wording in passage | Build paraphrase log; broad reading |
| Word-matching | Chose option containing same words but different meaning | Match whole meaning; rephrase the sentence |
| Missed keyword | Ignored "abroad", "currently", "most" etc. | Underline every keyword before scanning |
| False vs Not Given confusion | Chose NG when passage contradicts, or False when info absent | Apply the definitions strictly; check every part |
| Order assumption | Searched in wrong part of passage | Learn which types follow order |
| Time pressure | Rushed or left blanks | Timing drills; skip-and-return rule |
| Instruction error | Reused a letter when not allowed, exceeded word limit | Read instructions twice |
| Grammar/form error | Wrong plural, spelling, word form | Check the sentence after inserting answer |

If errors cluster in one cause, that is the focus for the next practice block.

## 6. Practice methodology (Liz's "analyse, don't accumulate" principle)

1. Do a small section of questions, not always a full test.
2. Stop and analyse **every** answer — right and wrong.
3. For each, identify the passage evidence and the paraphrase used.
4. Log recurring paraphrases and error causes.
5. Practise the problematic question type in isolation.
6. Only then do a full test under exam conditions to measure progress.
7. Use authentic IELTS Cambridge test books for real past papers; use other lessons for
   skill-building.

## 7. Common traps summary

- Matching words instead of meaning.
- Assuming paraphrase = one synonym.
- Not understanding the differences between question types.
- Chaotic approach instead of a repeatable technique.
- Doing test after test without analysis.
- Forgetting that spelling, grammar and word limits are marked.
- Mixing T/F/NG with Y/N/NG answer labels.
