# IELTS Reading: Question Types (14 practical types)

Key distinction: **"answers come in order"** means the answer to Q1 appears before Q2
in the passage. This is TRUE for most types, but NOT for matching headings, matching
paragraph information and matching features. Always read the instructions.

## 1. Matching headings
- **Task:** choose a heading for each paragraph/section from a list.
- **Skills:** identify the aim/main idea of a section; distinguish main idea from supporting detail.
- **Tips:** read the headings FIRST (they appear before the passage); there are usually more headings than needed; analyse the headings before matching; answers are Roman numerals (i, ii, iii) — check instructions; answers **do not come in order**; one heading per paragraph.
- **Trap:** choosing a heading based on one detail instead of the whole paragraph.

## 2. True / False / Not Given (identifying information)
- **Task:** decide whether a statement agrees with, contradicts, or is absent from the passage.
- **True:** same information found. **False:** passage gives different/contradicting information. **Not Given:** the whole meaning is not in the passage.
- **Tips:** analyse the statement before searching; paraphrase the statement; answers **come in order**; `T/F/NG` acceptable.
- **Trap:** Not Given vs False — some keywords may appear but the whole meaning may be absent; conversely, contradiction may be expressed with different words.

## 3. Yes / No / Not Given (identifying writer's views/claims)
- Same technique as T/F/NG, but about the **writer's opinions/claims**, not facts.
- **Never mix answer systems** (`Y/N/NG` only).
- Common in Academic; rare in GT.

## 4. Matching information (matching paragraph information)
- **Task:** match a statement to the paragraph containing that information.
- **Tips:** paraphrase the statement; answers **do not come in order**; answer is usually a letter (A, B, C...); not all paragraphs may be used; check whether a letter may be reused.
- **Trap:** confusing this with matching headings — headings are about the *main idea*; this is about a *specific detail*.

## 5. Summary / note / table / flow-chart completion
- **Task:** fill gaps using words from the passage or from a box.
- **Tips:** identify the part of speech needed for each gap; locate the information in order; check the word limit; the completed text must be **grammatically correct** — grammar helps you choose; watch word forms (singular/plural).
- **Trap:** copying a word that fits meaning but not grammar.

## 6. Sentence completion
- **Task:** complete sentences with words from the passage.
- **Tips:** identify word type; locate information; check word limit; grammar of the sentence must work; answers usually come in order.
- **Trap:** using a near-synonym instead of the exact word from the passage when instructed to use passage words.

## 7. Multiple choice
- **Task:** choose the correct option, usually A/B/C/D, for a question or sentence ending.
- **Tips:** paraphrase the question AND the options; locate the precise information; answers come in order; distractors often repeat words from the passage but change the meaning.
- **Trap:** choosing an option because it contains familiar words.

## 8. List selection
- **Task:** choose from one long list of names/words (usually A–G) which items match given descriptions.
- **Tips:** read the list and prepare paraphrases; identify keywords in the questions; answers come in order.
- **Trap:** assuming each option is used once — check instructions.

## 9. Choosing a title
- **Task:** pick the best title for the whole passage.
- **Tips:** compare the differences between titles; focus on opening and closing paragraphs; don't spend long — worth only 1 mark.
- **Trap:** choosing a title that covers only one paragraph.

## 10. Classification / categorisation (matching features)
- **Task:** decide which category a statement belongs to (e.g. which researcher made which claim).
- **Tips:** locate the information; watch for paraphrases; answers **do not come in order**.
- **Trap:** matching a name to a neighbouring sentence rather than the exact claim.

## 11. Matching sentence endings
- **Task:** match each sentence beginning with the correct ending from a list.
- **Tips:** read beginnings then endings; prepare paraphrases; find information in passage; the completed sentence must be grammatical; beginnings follow passage order; usually more endings than needed.
- **Trap:** ignoring grammar when two endings seem plausible.

## 12. Table completion
- **Task:** complete a table with words from the passage.
- **Tips:** read column headings first; identify word type for each cell; scan; check word limit.

## 13. Flow-chart completion
- **Task:** fill a flow chart from the passage.
- **Tips:** identify word type; follow arrow/box direction; answers **do not always come in order**; check word limit.

## 14. Diagram label completion
- **Task:** label a diagram (often a process or object).
- **Tips:** identify word type; information is usually in one or two specific paragraphs; answers do not always come in order; check word limit.

## 15. Short-answer questions
- **Task:** answer detail questions with words from the passage.
- **Tips:** identify required word type; paraphrase the question; scan; check word limit; answers come in order.

## Fast reference: order of answers

| Answers FOLLOW passage order | Answers DO NOT follow passage order |
|---|---|
| T/F/NG, Y/N/NG, multiple choice, list selection, sentence completion, summary completion, short answer, matching sentence endings (beginnings) | Matching headings, matching information (paragraphs), matching features/classification, flow-chart completion, diagram labels |

Use the "follows order" rule to narrow your search area and save time.
