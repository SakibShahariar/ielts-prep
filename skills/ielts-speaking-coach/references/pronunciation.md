# IELTS Speaking: Pronunciation (what examiners actually listen for)

Pronunciation is **not** about having a "neutral" accent. The official descriptors say an
accent has no effect on intelligibility at Band 9. It is about the phonological features you
control and how much effort the listener needs to understand you.

## The four official indicator areas

1. **Chunking** — dividing speech into meaningful utterances. Speak in thought groups, not
   word by word, and pause at the end of a spoken sentence (usually with falling intonation).
2. **Rhythm and stress-timing** — English is stress-timed: the time between stressed syllables
   is roughly even. Function words reduce; content words carry stress. A rapid, flat rate
   often destroys rhythm (a Band 6 problem).
3. **Connected speech** — linking, elision and reduction: `want to → wanna` in natural speech,
   `going to → gonna`, `next day → nex' day`. At Band 9 flexible use of connected speech is sustained.
4. **Stress and intonation for meaning** — emphatic/contrastive stress (`I said *Tuesday*, not
   Monday`) and intonation that signals attitude, stages and unfinished vs finished thoughts.

Plus **word/phoneme level**: word stress, vowel and consonant production. Mispronouncing
individual words only matters when it causes lack of clarity.

## Band 8 vs Band 9 pronunciation

- **Band 8:** wide range of phonological features; sustained rhythm; flexible stress and
  intonation across long utterances despite occasional lapses; easily understood; accent minimal effect.
- **Band 9:** full range; flexible connected speech sustained throughout; **effortlessly**
  understood; accent no effect.

The jump is from "easy to understand" to "effortless", and from good chunks to sustained
control across long turns.

## Drills

- **Shadowing:** listen to a short native-speaker clip and speak along with it, copying
  chunking, stress and intonation — not just words.
- **Marked script:** take your own model answer, mark `/` for pauses, underline stressed words,
  then read it aloud with the marked rhythm.
- **Minimal pairs:** `ship/sheep`, `live/leave`, `bad/bed`, `thirty/thirteen` — record yourself.
- **Number and name drills:** dates, prices, phone numbers, spelled names — clarity under pressure.
- **Recording:** record 60 seconds of your answers weekly; check for chunking, filler overload
  and words you have to repeat for clarity.

## Common problems and fixes

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Speaking too fast, losing clarity | Slow down slightly; stress content words; pause between thought groups |
| Flat intonation | Exaggerate pitch movement in practice; mark rising/falling in your script |
| Word-stress errors | Learn stress with each new word (`PHOtograph`, `phoTOGraphy`) |
| Too many fillers | Replace with a short silent pause while planning content |
| Mispronounced familiar words | Keep a personal error list from your recordings |
