# IELTS Speaking: Format and Scoring

Source base: official IELTS partners (ielts.org) — Speaking test format, Speaking Band
Descriptors and Key Assessment Criteria; IELTS Liz. Verified November 2026.

## 1. Format

- **One Speaking test for all candidates** — Academic and General Training take the same test with the same marking.
- **Face-to-face interview with an examiner** (or via video call). **The test is recorded.**
- **11–14 minutes**, three parts. The examiner controls the timing and decides when to move on.

| Part | Name | Duration | Task |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction and interview | 4–5 min | ~12 short questions on ~3 everyday topics (home, hometown, family, hobbies, etc.) |
| 2 | Long turn | 3–4 min total | Cue card with bullet points; **1 minute** preparation with paper and pen; speak **1–2 minutes** (aim for 2); then 1–2 rounding-off questions |
| 3 | Discussion | 4–5 min | ~5–6 questions on one topic related to Part 2, more abstract; examiner may challenge answers |

## 2. Marking

- **Four criteria, equally weighted (25% each):** Fluency and Coherence · Lexical Resource ·
  Grammatical Range and Accuracy · Pronunciation.
- The examiner who administers the test decides the score **after the test ends**.
- You are **not marked per part** — the score reflects your performance across the whole test.
- The recording allows a **remark by a senior examiner** if requested.
- IELTS defines the band as the operator's overall proficiency level; candidate's target is
  Band 8–9 here. There is no raw-score conversion for Speaking.
- Topics are recycled and are only a vehicle to showcase language — the examiner is not
  testing knowledge or opinions.
- The test is **informal**: informal language is normal and appropriate.

## 3. Key assessment criteria (official definitions)

- **Fluency & Coherence:** continuity, rate and effort; logical sequencing; clear marking of
  stages; relevance; cohesive devices.
- **Lexical Resource:** variety; adequacy/appropriacy (referential meaning, style, collocation,
  attitude); ability to paraphrase around a gap.
- **Grammatical Range & Accuracy:** length of spoken sentences; subordinate clauses; complex
  verb phrases; pre/post-modification; error density and communicative effect.
- **Pronunciation:** chunking; rhythm and stress-timing; linking/elision; emphatic stress and
  intonation; word and phoneme production; effect of accent on intelligibility.

## 4. Band 8 vs Band 9 (the target zone)

| Criterion | **Band 8** | **Band 9** |
|---|---|---|
| Fluency & Coherence | Fluent with only very occasional repetition/self-correction; hesitation may occasionally be used to find words or grammar, but most is content-related; topic development coherent, appropriate and relevant | Fluent with only very occasional repetition/self-correction; hesitation used **only** to prepare content, not to find words or grammar; speech situationally appropriate; topic development fully coherent and appropriately extended |
| Lexical Resource | Wide resource readily and flexibly used; skilful less common/idiomatic items despite occasional inaccuracies; effective paraphrase | Total flexibility and precise use in all contexts; sustained accurate and idiomatic language |
| Grammar | Wide range flexibly used; majority of sentences error-free; occasional non-systematic errors; a few basic errors may persist | Structures precise and accurate at all times, apart from mistakes characteristic of native speaker speech |
| Pronunciation | Wide range of phonological features; sustained rhythm; flexible stress and intonation across long utterances despite occasional lapses; easily understood; accent minimal effect | Full range of phonological features; flexible connected speech sustained throughout; **effortlessly understood**; accent no effect on intelligibility |
