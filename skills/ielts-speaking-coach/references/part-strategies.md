# IELTS Speaking: Part Strategies

## Part 1 — Introduction and interview (4–5 min, ~12 questions, ~3 topics)

**Goal:** quick, natural, expanded answers. A one-word answer wastes a chance to show language.

**Answer shape: Answer → Reason/Detail → Example (2–4 sentences, ~15–30 seconds).**
- *"I live in Dhaka, in the north of the city. It's quite crowded, but I like it because everything is within walking distance."*
- Give a direct answer first, then one reason or detail. Do not over-extend — the examiner controls the pace and may interrupt.

**Tactics:** prepare everyday topics (home, hometown, family, work/study, hobbies, food, travel,
sleep, colours, happiness). Rehearse the *content* and useful phrases, never memorised sentences.
Use contractions and informal language naturally. If you don't know a word, paraphrase immediately.

**Traps:** one-word answers; memorised monologues; over-formal register; over-use of fillers.

## Part 2 — Long turn (1 min prep, 1–2 min talk)

**Goal:** talk for the full 1–2 minutes (aim for 2) with clear structure and no dead silence.

**Preparation (1 minute):** note 3–4 keywords covering **all** bullet points. Add one example
and one personal detail. Do not write a script.

**Talk structure:**
1. **Introduction** — introduce the topic in one sentence.
2. **Body** — cover each cue-card bullet in order, extending each with detail and an example.
3. **Conclusion** — why it matters / how it made you feel / a final personal reflection.

**Extension techniques:**
- **PREP:** Point → Reason → Example → Point again.
- **Past / present / future:** what happened, what it's like now, what will happen next.
- **Describe + explain + evaluate:** facts, then why, then your judgement.
- **Personal angle:** "What made it memorable for me was..."

**Tactics:** use natural signposting (`To begin with`, `What I found interesting was`, `Looking back`).
If you finish early, add a personal reflection rather than stopping abruptly. Not reaching 2
minutes is not an automatic penalty, but less talking means less evidence to show language.

**Traps:** ignoring a bullet point; going off topic; memorising a story that doesn't fit the cue;
panic-silence; over-long pauses.

## Part 3 — Discussion (4–5 min, ~5–6 questions)

**Goal:** develop abstract ideas with clear positions, examples and nuance.

**Answer shape: Position → Explanation → Example → Contrast/Nuance → Conclusion.**
- Acknowledge complexity: `It depends on...`, `There's no simple answer, but...`, `While some people would say X, I'd argue Y because...`
- Give reasons and evidence; speculate (`This could lead to...`, `One possible consequence is...`).
- Compare and evaluate (`compared with`, `the main drawback is`, `in the long run`).

**Tactics:** prepare thematic vocabulary for common Part 3 areas (technology, education, health,
environment, work, transport, crime, globalisation, culture). Use hedging (`may`, `tends to`,
`in many cases`) — it is a Band 8+ feature.

**Traps:** giving yes/no without development; repeating Part 2 content; personal anecdotes when
the question asks for general views; treating examiner challenges as criticism.

## Fillers and hesitation

- Small fillers (`Well`, `I mean`, `Let me think`, `That's an interesting one`) are natural and
  acceptable when used to **plan content**.
- At Band 9, hesitation is used only to prepare content, **not to search for words or grammar**.
- Avoid excessive `umm`, `er`, false starts and backtracking. A short pause is better than a
  string of fillers.
- Self-correction is fine occasionally; frequent self-correction lowers Fluency.

## Paraphrase (Lexical Resource)

When you don't know a word, explain it: "It's the thing you use to..." / "It's a bit like..."
This is explicitly rewarded. Effective paraphrase prevents a fluency breakdown.

## The examiner interrupting

Interruption is normal — especially in Part 3 — and is not a penalty. Stop when the examiner
speaks and answer the new question. The examiner controls timing, not you.

## Topic preparation

Topics are recycled. Build a topic bank of 25–30 familiar topics with:
- 5–8 useful words/collocations each
- one personal story per topic (reusable across Part 2 prompts)
- 2–3 Part 3 opinions with reasons

Model answers are for learning language and length — **never memorise them word for word**.
