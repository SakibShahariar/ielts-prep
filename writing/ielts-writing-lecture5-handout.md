## 1. Essay type 3 — Causes and Effects / Causes and Solutions

**Handout rule:** discuss the **causes or reasons** of a problem; the likely **effects** may feature
separately in the second half. Find relevant points for both. **Do not add a solutions paragraph**
unless the question asks for solutions. If the question asks causes **and solutions**, you do **not**
need to discuss effects — that is not the focus.

**Topic:** *Obesity in children is a serious problem in a number of countries. What are the causes
of obesity in children? Discuss the possible alarming effects of this problem in the future.*

**Structure:**
- **Introduction:** background statement · paraphrase · overview statement giving an idea of the body.
- **Body:** three body paragraphs in the handout — one for causes, one each for good and bad
  effects. Topic sentences highlight the main focus; one or two points per paragraph, supported
  by examples/facts.
- **Conclusion:** rewrite the overview; summarise; no new points.

> ⚠️ **Band 8–9 correction:** answer **exactly** what is asked. If the prompt asks only for
> alarming effects, do not invent "good effects" to fill a third paragraph. Two or three body
> paragraphs are both fine — what matters is that every part is answered and developed.

**Handout sample (sleep), condensed to Band 8–9:**

> People generally sleep less today than in the past. This can be traced to modern technology and
> the pressures of modern life, and the consequences are largely adverse.
>
> One major cause is technology. Before smartphones and tablets, people often read or watched TV
> before bed; now many spend hours staring at bright screens whose engaging content makes it hard
> to fall asleep. Another factor is early starts: young people may stay up late on their phones
> yet have to be at school by 6 a.m., while adults working 40-hour weeks often spend late hours on
> recreation, reducing their sleep further.
>
> The effects can be severe. Sleep deprivation lowers energy and makes concentration at work or
> school difficult. It also affects those around us: tired friends and family may lose their temper
> or act without thinking. In the most serious cases, fatigue causes road accidents — thousands
> every year involve sleepy drivers.
>
> In conclusion, lack of sleep is mainly caused by new technology and modern pressures, and its
> effects range from mild irritation to fatal accidents.

**Cause-and-solution variant** (handout example: youth crime — causes = negative family
environment, pressure to succeed; solutions = parents paying attention, encouraging children to
study and develop confidence). The conclusion may include a recommendation, but no new ideas.

## 2. Essay type 4 — Comparison and Contrast (discussion)

Unlike an argumentative essay, you may stay in the middle **or** take a side, based on your points.

**Topic:** *Some people believe adults are motivated to exercise by major sports events on TV such
as the Olympics and the Football World Cup. Others say there are many ways apart from TV to
encourage them to exercise. Discuss both views and give your opinion.*

**Structure:** introduction (background · paraphrase · thesis giving your notion) → two body
paragraphs, one for each view, with topic sentences and supporting examples → conclusion that
restates your opinion briefly, says why, summarises, and adds no new points.

**Second sample topic:** *Nowadays people make new friends through social networks and internet
chat groups. Some think this is good; others think face-to-face interaction is essential. Discuss
both views and give your opinion.*

**Band 8–9 note:** the handout samples for these essays contain several garbled sentences
(e.g. *"converse about the things they enjoy, while sharing worldwide"*). Do not copy their
phrasing — use the clean structure and your own accurate language.

**Band 8–9 model conclusion for the social-media topic:**
> In conclusion, online friendships are a positive development, but they cannot replace the warmth
> of physical contact. Social media works best as a supplement to face-to-face interaction, which
> remains essential for personal growth and emotional fulfilment.

## 3. Essay type 5 — Double Question

An argumentative question plus a second question (often advantages/disadvantages, causes/solutions
or effects). **Both questions must be addressed with equal importance and space** — failing to do
so means not fulfilling the task requirements and a low score.

**Topic:** *Some parents buy their children whatever they ask for and allow them to do whatever
they want. Is this a good way to raise children? What consequences could this style of parenting
have as they get older?*

**Structure:**
- **Introduction:** background · paraphrase · thesis giving your opinion **and** a brief idea of the other question.
- **Body:** one paragraph answering the opinion question (topic sentence, reasons, examples, one
  counter-argument refuted); one paragraph answering the second question (topic sentence, one or
  two supported points).
- **Conclusion:** restate your opinion briefly and why; summarise; no new points.

**Clean Band 8–9 model (advertising prompt):**
> *These days, consumers face an increasing amount of advertising. To what extent are they
> influenced by it? What measures should be taken to protect them?*
>
> Advertising has become an increasingly pervasive presence in modern life. I believe it
> significantly influences consumers, and that strict regulation is needed to prevent it becoming
> manipulative.
>
> Shoppers are influenced because advertising exploits vulnerabilities in human psychology.
> Agencies have refined strategies such as the "fear of missing out" and limited-time promotions,
> and the "Black Friday" phenomenon shows how effectively these techniques drive demand. Some
> argue that advertising simply informs people about products; however, the industry's scale and
> its targeting of vulnerable groups suggest the influence goes well beyond information.
>
> To protect consumers, advertising should be strictly regulated by the state. Products that are
> naturally addictive, such as junk foods high in sugar and fat, should not be marketed to
> children. Legislation could also prevent companies from using private online data to target
> individuals, and from placing marketing near schools and playgrounds.
>
> In conclusion, advertising exerts considerable influence, and regulation is essential as
> marketing becomes increasingly sophisticated.

## 4. Examiner's comments on a Band 5 essay (diagnostic gold)

**Prompt:** *There are social, medical and technical problems associated with mobile phones. What
forms do they take? Do you agree that the problems outweigh the benefits?*

A sample response begins: *"Mobile phone is a important technology people use it for tremendous
convenience. Obviously, it makes some social, medical and technical problems. However, its pros far
outweigh its cons."*

**Examiner's remarks (from the handout):**
- Addresses the question appropriately; range of ideas; position given in the opening paragraph.
- Ideas organised decently, but the **third paragraph is too short and confusing**.
- Cohesive devices attempted (`However`, `In addition`) but **mostly not in appropriate places**;
  **referencing not used appropriately** (`they`, `it`).
- Vocabulary is **basic**, with few errors; redundant uses of vocabulary.
- Grammar range is **basic**; the level of error means the band **cannot be higher than 5.0**.

**Band 8–9 lesson:** ideas alone are not enough. Precision, appropriate linking, referencing,
vocabulary range and grammatical control decide the band. Fix articles (`an important technology`),
avoid run-on sentences, and use referencing (`this trend`, `such devices`, `the former`).

## 5. Editing checklist (from the handout)

After writing, use the final minutes to check:

1. Accurate grammatical structures — consistent tense, subject–verb agreement.
2. Word formation (nouns, verbs, adjectives); correct use of `a/an`, `the` and prepositions.
3. A range of sentence structures.
4. Appropriate vocabulary.
5. Accurate spelling.
6. Main idea for each paragraph in a topic sentence; all points related to the topic.
7. Connective words used effectively to link ideas logically.
8. Each paragraph adequately developed.
9. Enough detailed information and sufficient examples or facts.
10. A definite point of view developed.
11. Every paragraph helps address the task.

## 6. Useful language patterns (from the handout)

**Expressing views:** `I would like to say that...` · `I think that...` · `As far as I understand/know...` ·
`I firmly believe/trust that...` · `In my knowledge...` · `People argue that...` ·
`Some people think/say that...` · `It is understood that...` · `It is generally accepted that...`

**Hedging/linking:** `while` · `although` · `despite` · `admittedly` · `certainly`

**Refuting:** `I am unconvinced that...` · `I don't believe that...` · `It is hard to accept that...` ·
`There is little evidence to support the view that...` · `It is unjustifiable to say that...`

**Defining/explaining:** `By... I mean...` · `By this I mean...` · `In other words,...` ·
`That is to say,...` · `To be more precise,...` · `Here I'm (not) referring to...`

**Providing support:** `For example,` · `For instance,` · `Indeed,` · `In fact,` ·
`A good example of this is...` · `If this were the case,...` · `Firstly,` · `Naturally,` ·
`Of course,` · `In my experience,` · `Let me illustrate...`

**Comparison / contrast:** `almost the same as` · `common to` · `compared with` · `in the same way` ·
`just as` · `like / alike` · `resemblance` · `similar to` | `different from` · `even so` ·
`however` · `in contrast to` · `in opposition to` · `less than` · `more than` · `otherwise`

**Cause and effect:** `accordingly` · `as a result` · `because` · `because of` · `consequently` ·
`due to` · `for this reason` · `have an effect on` · `hence` · `owing to` · `reason for` · `since` ·
`so` · `therefore` · `thus`

**Concluding expressions:** `therefore` · `hence` · `consequently` · `proves that` · `as a result` ·
`which shows that` · `we may infer that` · `which implies that` · `which entails that` ·
`so accordingly` · `for this reason / these reasons` · `in consequence` · `it follows that` ·
`which means that` · `which points to the conclusion that`

## 7. Band 8–9 checklist from Lecture 5

- [ ] Causes/effects: answer causes and effects; add solutions **only** if asked
- [ ] Comparison/contrast: both views explained, opinion given, balanced or one-sided
- [ ] Double question: **both** questions answered with equal weight
- [ ] Topic sentence first in every body paragraph; one central topic per paragraph
- [ ] Ideas extended and supported; examples clear, concise, realistic
- [ ] Referencing used (`this trend`, `such devices`) rather than loose `they`/`it`
- [ ] Cohesive devices in appropriate places, not scattered
- [ ] Editing checklist completed in the final 5 minutes
- [ ] 270–290 words in ~40 minutes
