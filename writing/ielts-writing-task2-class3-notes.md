> **B** — **Discussion type**: "Discuss both views and give your opinion" (driving age).

---

## 1. The new lesson: Discussion type

For **"Discuss both views and give your opinion"**, the structure is different from an opinion essay:

| Paragraph | Rule |
|---|---|
| **1. Introduction** | Paraphrase **both** views. **No opinion here.** |
| **2. Body 1** | View 1 — topic sentence + Point → Explanation → Example → Result |
| **3. Body 2** | View 2 — same structure |
| **4. Conclusion** | Conclusion word → your opinion → `view 1 → view 2 → view 1` → `not only... but also` / `as well as` → suggestion |

Useful twist words for linking the two views: `that` · `however` · `whereas`.

⚠️ Avoid meta-commentary such as *"an attempt will be made to examine both perspectives"* or
*"this viewpoint will be further explained to support a well-founded conclusion"*. A brief
*"This essay will examine both views before presenting my opinion"* is acceptable, but simply
paraphrasing the two views is stronger and more natural.

## 2. Essay A — Nuclear energy

**Prompt**
> *Nuclear energy is better than other energy resources in meeting the ever-increasing needs
> of the globe. To what extent do you agree or disagree?*

### Band 8–9 model answer

Nuclear energy is often presented as a sustainable and comparatively clean solution to the
world's growing energy needs. While it avoids the depletion of fossil fuels and emits little
carbon dioxide, I argue that its high costs and serious safety risks mean it is not superior
to other energy sources.

On the one hand, I understand why many people advocate building more nuclear power stations.
Primarily, nuclear power generates electricity without depleting scarce non-renewable resources
such as coal, oil and gas, which are becoming increasingly limited. As a result, it is widely
regarded as a promising option for conserving these resources for future generations.
Furthermore, nuclear plants emit far less carbon dioxide and fewer toxic substances than
coal-fired plants. By using nuclear resources, countries can reduce the emissions that drive
global warming.

On the contrary, nuclear energy carries significant dangers. First and foremost, the primary
concern is the potential for a radiation accident with long-lasting detrimental effects on
both people and the environment. A notable example is the Fukushima disaster of 2011, which
forced the evacuation of thousands of people from their homes. The possibility of such a
catastrophe makes nuclear power a poor choice for the future. Additionally, construction is
extremely expensive: a single plant can cost billions of dollars and places a heavy financial
burden on governments.

In conclusion, while nuclear power is both powerful and comparatively clean, the associated
risks and high costs are considerable. I firmly believe that governments should prioritise
safer and more affordable alternatives such as wind and solar power. Ultimately, however, the
choice comes down to which risks a society is willing to accept.

*(≈270 words)*

## 3. Essay B — Driving age (Discussion type)

**Prompt**
> *Most countries allow 18-year-olds to start driving a car. Some say it is good to allow
> driving at that age. Others think that the age to start driving should be at least 25 years.
> Discuss both views and give your opinion.*

### Band 8–9 model answer

In most countries, eighteen-year-olds are permitted to drive, but some people believe the
minimum age should be raised to 25. There are strong arguments on both sides of this debate.

On the one hand, numerous people support allowing 18-year-olds to drive. Firstly, it gives
young adults a valuable sense of independence: they can commute to university or work without
relying on their parents, and this helps them become self-reliant. Moreover, driving opens up
better employment opportunities and enables young people to support their families. In rural
areas, where public transport such as buses and trains is limited, a car can be essential for
daily life. Hence, being able to drive at 18 allows young people to explore a wider range of
opportunities.

On the other hand, some believe that 25 is a more appropriate age for obtaining a licence.
Primarily, older individuals tend to be more mature and remain calmer in traffic, which
reduces the likelihood of fatal accidents. Furthermore, many collisions are caused by speeding
and distraction — problems that are more common among younger drivers. Raising the legal
driving age to 25 could therefore result in fewer road fatalities. Consequently, governments
would spend less on healthcare, and young people would be less likely to face a tragedy early
in life.

In conclusion, while raising the driving age would undoubtedly improve road safety and reduce
accidents, the freedom to travel at 18 and the opportunities it creates are also important.
Provided that strict testing and training are in place, I believe 18 remains the right age to
start driving, as it enables young adults to become responsible drivers.

*(≈265 words)*

| `an attempt will be made to examine both perspective` | Meta + passive + number | delete / paraphrase both views |
| `letting 18-year-olds to drive` | Bare infinitive | `letting 18-year-olds drive` |
| `young adult` | Number | `young adults` |
| `self-dependent` | Wrong collocation | `self-reliant` |
| `some believes` | Agreement | `some believe` |

## 4. Reusable sentence bank from class 3

| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Agree/disagree intro** | *While X is beneficial, I argue that Y means it is not superior to...* |
| **Discussion intro (no opinion)** | *There are strong arguments on both sides of this debate.* |
| **View 1** | *On the one hand, numerous people support... · Primarily,... · As a result,...* |
| **View 2** | *On the other hand, some believe that... · Furthermore,... · Consequently,...* |
| **Example** | *A notable example is... · which resulted in...* |
| **Opinion in conclusion** | *I firmly believe that... · Provided that..., I believe... · Ultimately, the choice comes down to...* |
| **Final line** | *Ultimately, the preference depends on the individual's priorities.* |

## 5. Band 8–9 checklist

- [ ] For discussion type, **no opinion in paragraph 1**
- [ ] Both views given a full body paragraph with Point → Explanation → Example → Result
- [ ] Opinion in the conclusion: view 1 → view 2 → view 1
- [ ] Exact question answered (`to what extent` → degree of agreement; `discuss both` → both views)
- [ ] Examples specific (`Fukushima 2011`, `rural areas with limited buses/trains`)
- [ ] Precise collocations (`deplete resources`, `long-lasting detrimental effects`, `road fatalities`)
- [ ] Majority of sentences error-free — no `some believes`, `letting... to drive`, fragments
- [ ] No meta-commentary lines
- [ ] 265–285 words, 4 paragraphs
