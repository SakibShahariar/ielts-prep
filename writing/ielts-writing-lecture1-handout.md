# IELTS Writing — Mentors Lecture 1 Handout (Band 8–9 Reference)

> Extracted and upgraded from the **Mentors' IELTS Writing Lecture 1 — Student Copy** handout.
> Covers test mechanics, question types, the official assessment criteria, opening frames,
> reporting vocabulary, percentage vocabulary and practice charts. Handout sample texts contain
> a few inaccuracies — corrected below.

---

## 1. Test mechanics (handout overview)

- Academic Module: **two tasks** — Task 1 and Task 2.
- Writing test = **60 minutes**; advised **~20 minutes Task 1**, **~40 minutes Task 2**.
- **Task 1: at least 150 words. Task 2: at least 250 words.**
- **Task 1 = 1/3 of the Writing score; Task 2 = 2/3.**
- Task 1 in Academic: describe facts/figures in graphs, charts or tables on a related topic,
  or explain how a machine/device/process works. Include the **most important and most relevant**
  points; minor details may be omitted. Academic or semi-formal/neutral style.
- No penalty for writing over 150 words — but a longer Task 1 leaves less time for Task 2,
  which is worth twice as much.
- Penalties: irrelevance/off-topic; not full connected text (bullets, note form);
  **severely penalised: plagiarism** (copying from another source).
- The task assesses ability to identify key information and trends, and give a **well-organised
  overview** accurately in an academic register — essentially a **report**, not an essay.

## 2. The exact Task 1 instruction

> *You should spend about 20 minutes on this task. Summarise the information by selecting and
> reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.*

## 3. Assessment criteria (Task 1)

| Criterion | How responses are assessed |
|---|---|
| **Task Achievement** | How appropriately, accurately and relevantly the candidate fulfils the task requirements |
| **Coherence & Cohesion** | Overall clarity and fluency; organisation and linking of information and ideas; logical sequencing and appropriate linking devices within and between sentences |
| **Lexical Resource** | Range of vocabulary; accuracy and appropriacy in relation to the specific task |
| **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** | Range and accurate use of grammar at sentence level |

**The examiner checks whether you can:** recognise different graphical representations ·
isolate the main subjects · describe basic features · describe, sort and compare data ·
describe trends · draw inferences.

## 5. Writing the opening (introduction)

From the handout:
- Make **general statements**; save details for later.
- **Do not copy word for word** — paraphrase in your own words.
- Four opening prompts:
  1. *The graph shows / illustrates the trends in... between... and...*
  2. *The graph gives / provides / reveals / presents information about (the differences / changes...)*
  3. *The graph compares / contrasts...*
  4. *The graph shows / illustrates how the sales have differed / changed.*
- **Vary the noun phrase** (subject of reporting): `sales/purchases of different cars` ·
  `sales/purchases of private vehicles` · `the number of various types of cars sold/purchased` ·
  `car sales/purchases`.

**Band 8–9 upgrade:** state the unit and period in the introduction too:
*The bar chart compares the proportion of students involved in three sports across three school
classes. Figures are given as percentages.*

## 6. Basic vocabulary for reporting (from the handout diagram)

| Meaning | Verbs | Nouns |
|---|---|---|
| **Rise** | increased · rose · climbed · shot up · rocketed · surged · soared | an increase · a climb · a jump · a leap · a surge |
| **Fall** | collapsed · declined · decreased · deteriorated · dipped · dived · dropped · fell (back) · went down · plummeted · plunged · slid · slipped · slumped · took a nosedive | a decline · a decrease · a deterioration · a dip · a drop · a plunge · a free-fall · a slide · a reduction · a slump |
| **Fluctuate** | rose and fell erratically / sporadically / irregularly / intermittently / unevenly | fluctuations |
| **Flat** | — | no change · constant · unchanged · stable |
| **High point** | peaked · apexed · reached a zenith | a peak · an apex · a zenith |
| **Stable after change** | plateaued · stabilised · levelled off · remained constant | a plateau · stability |
| **Low point** | bottomed out | a low · a bottom · a nadir |

⚠️ Handout note: **`reduce` is only used in the passive** for data (`the figure was reduced by 10%`),
not `the figure reduced by 10%`.

**Adverbs:** dramatically · erratically · gradually · markedly · slightly · slowly · steadily ·
minimally · significantly · considerably · substantially · noticeably · remarkably.

**Data ranges:** `(increasing etc) from... to...` · `between... and...` ·
`with an increase from... to...`

**Add information with:** `followed by...`

**Time phrases:** `between... and...` · `from... to... (inclusive)` · `at... / in...` · `by...` ·
`in (e.g. 1994)...` · `during / over the period...` · `over the latter half of the year / century /
decade / period` · `over the next / past / previous five days / weeks / months / years / decades / era`.

## 7. Linkers, focus and predictions

- **Linkers:** `however` · `in contrast` · `by comparison` · `meanwhile` · `on the other hand`
- **To focus on one item:** `As regards...(sales), they...` · `With regard to / Regarding / In the
  case of / As for / Turning to...(sales), they...` · `Where... is concerned, they...` ·
  `When it comes to..., they...`
- **Predictions:** `It is predicted / forecast / estimated / expected / projected / anticipated
  that... will...` · `... will... by...` · `The projection is for... by...` ·
  `... is/are predicted/forecast/estimated/expected/projected/anticipated to...` · `... is/are set to...`

## 8. Percentage vocabulary

| Percentage | Fraction |
|---|---|
| 80% | four-fifths |
| 75% | three-quarters |
| 50% | half |
| 25% | a quarter |
| 20% | a fifth |
| 10% | one in ten |

**Qualifiers:** 77% → *just over three quarters / approximately three quarters* ·
48% → *just under a half / nearly a half* · 34% → *almost a third*.
Ranges: 75–85% → *a very large majority* · 65–75% → *a significant proportion* ·
10–15% → *a minority* · 5% → *a very small number*.

**Band 8–9 note:** use fractions sparingly and only when they aid precision; never substitute a
fraction that changes the meaning (48% is *nearly half*, not *half*).

## 9. Practice exercises — Band 8–9 answers

### Exercise 1 — rewrite "Alentejo had the highest percentage of visitors" four ways

1. Alentejo attracted the highest proportion of visitors, at 25%.
2. The largest visitor share was recorded in Alentejo, accounting for a quarter of the total.
3. Alentejo was the most visited of the nine locations, with 25% of all visitors.
4. Visitors to Alentejo outnumbered those to any other destination, reaching 25%.

⚠️ Handout sample correction: it claims *"Paros and Doha had equal visitor percentages of 10%"*
and *"Province... around 3%"*, but the chart shows roughly 15–16% and about 4% respectively.
Always read the chart, not the sample text.

### Exercise 2 — overview for *Changes in number of viewers for different movie genres in USA (1980–2020)*

> Overall, thriller was the most popular genre across the period and ended higher than it began,
> while horror and romantic films consistently attracted the smallest audiences. Comedy and
> science fiction both peaked in 2000 before falling back by 2020, whereas action viewers rose
> slightly and then levelled off.

### Exercise 3 — compare proportions from *US Spending Patterns, 2018*

> Cars accounted for nearly half of all spending, at 48%, making them by far the largest category.
> Recreation and food represented 13% and 12% respectively, while restaurants, petrol and
> furniture each made up roughly 7–8%. Books was the smallest item at just 4%.

## 10. Band 8–9 checklist from the handout

- [ ] Report, not essay — overview present, no conclusion
- [ ] Key features selected; minor details omitted
- [ ] Comparisons made where relevant
- [ ] Intro paraphrased, units stated, no copying
- [ ] Vocabulary varied — verbs, nouns, adverbs, fractions
- [ ] Full connected text — no bullets or notes
- [ ] 165–185 words in ~20 minutes
