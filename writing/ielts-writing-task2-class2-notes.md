## 1. What class 2 reinforced

- **Advantage vs disadvantage / outweigh prompts** — answer the exact question: *"Do the
  advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"* → give an explicit verdict.
- **Positive or negative development** → state clearly which one, and keep it consistent.
- Thesis pattern: *Although X is [benefit], Y is [drawback]. Therefore, from my perspective, [verdict].*
- Body 1: `On the one hand, there are clear benefits...`
- Body 2: `On the other hand, significant drawbacks remain...`
- Support: `Firstly` · `Furthermore` · `To cite an example` · `As a result` · `Consequently`
- Conclusion: `In conclusion, while..., ...` → `Hence, I firmly believe that...`

⚠️ **Exam rule:** never write *"I will elaborate on this viewpoint and provide a reasoned
conclusion"* or *"before arriving at a justified final thought"*. Those are comments about
your essay, not content. Your teacher uses them as classroom scaffolding — leave them out.

## 2. Essay A — watching live performances at home

**Prompt**
> *These days, many people like to watch live performances (such as concerts and shows) at
> home, either on TV or on a computer, instead of attending in person. Do the advantages of
> this trend outweigh its disadvantages?*

### Band 8–9 model answer

These days, many people prefer watching concerts and shows on television or on a computer
rather than attending in person. While this option is undeniably convenient and far cheaper,
I argue that the loss of atmosphere and the poorer quality of the experience outweigh these
benefits.

On the one hand, there are clear benefits to watching live performances from home. Firstly,
it is incredibly convenient: viewers do not have to travel long distances, queue for entry or
arrange transport home, so they can remain in their own living room and save a considerable
amount of time. Furthermore, digital viewing is far more affordable. Tickets for major shows
are often prohibitively expensive, whereas streaming allows anyone to enjoy the arts without
spending a fortune. As a result, people can save money on tickets, transport and food while
still accessing world-class performances from their own devices.

On the other hand, significant drawbacks remain. First and foremost, the key loss is the
absence of a genuine crowd atmosphere. At a live show, the energy exchanged between performers
and audience creates a unique experience that cannot be replicated on a screen, resulting in a
weaker emotional connection. Additionally, the limited sound quality of small speakers cannot
compare with the powerful audio systems and light displays of a real venue. For instance, a
rock concert in a stadium offers high-fidelity sound and dazzling lighting that engage both
the ears and the whole body. Watching the same event on a laptop reduces it to a diluted
version of the artist's original intention.

In conclusion, while watching from home is comfortable and economical, the lack of genuine
atmosphere and the inferior sensory experience make it a lesser experience. I firmly believe
that being present in person remains essential, and that the merits of home viewing do not
outweigh the drawbacks. Ultimately, the preference depends on the individual's priorities.

*(≈275 words)*

## 3. Essay B — fast-food sponsorship in schools

**Prompt**
> *Nowadays, marketing has left no stone unturned to entice customers. Certainly, fast food
> companies and restaurants now tie up with schools to endorse their products. Do you think
> this is a positive or negative development?*

### Band 8–9 model answer

Nowadays, marketing has left no stone unturned in its effort to entice customers, and
fast-food companies increasingly partner with schools to promote their products. Although
such deals provide schools with additional funding and relieve logistical pressure, I argue
that they harm children's health and undermine the educational mission of schools. This is
therefore a negative development.

On the one hand, some people argue that these partnerships are clearly beneficial. Firstly,
financial contributions from food giants can be used to fund extracurricular activities or
upgrade classroom technology. For example, institutions can offer scholarships to students in
need using this money. Consequently, schools with limited budgets can provide resources that
would otherwise be unaffordable. Furthermore, several fast-food companies organise catering
services that reduce the logistical burden on school administrators. The school can then focus
more on academic management while ensuring a consistent food supply for the student body.

On the other hand, multiple drawbacks are associated with this trend. First and foremost,
children need access to healthy food in order to grow and learn effectively. Studies indicate
that students who consume excessive sugar and fat struggle to concentrate in class, so
introducing junk food into schools contradicts children's nutritional needs. Furthermore,
daily exposure to these brands can instil poor eating habits and encourage impulsive purchasing
decisions. Consequently, such behaviour may lead to health problems such as obesity and
diabetes later in life.

In conclusion, while the additional revenue and logistical support are undoubtedly appealing
to schools, the detrimental effects on students' health are far more consequential. I firmly
believe this is a negative development. Schools should focus on maintaining a proper learning
environment and the wellbeing of children rather than providing platforms for corporations to
maximise their profits.

*(≈265 words)*

## 4. Reusable thesis frames from class 2

| Prompt type | Frame |
|---|---|
| Advantages outweigh? | *While X is undeniably convenient and cheaper, I argue that the drawbacks outweigh these benefits.* |
| Positive/negative? | *Although X can provide A and B, it harms C. Therefore, from my perspective, this is a negative development.* |
| Outweigh (opposite verdict) | *While the benefits are considerable, I firmly believe the disadvantages are more significant.* |

**Body and conclusion linkers used here:** `Firstly` · `Furthermore` · `As a result` ·
`First and foremost` · `Additionally` · `For instance` · `Consequently` · `In conclusion` ·
`Hence` · `Ultimately`

## 5. Band 8–9 checklist for these two essays

- [ ] Exact question answered in thesis and conclusion (`outweigh?` → explicit verdict)
- [ ] Position consistent throughout — no drifting to the other side
- [ ] Both sides covered, but one clearly developed further to match the verdict
- [ ] Each body idea developed: Point → Explanation → Example → Result
- [ ] Specific examples (`streaming`, `scholarships`, `obesity and diabetes`)
- [ ] Precise collocations (`outweigh the drawbacks`, `undermine the mission`, `instil habits`)
- [ ] Majority of sentences error-free; no `super`, `do not surpass`, `far consequential`
- [ ] No meta-commentary lines about "elaborating" or "arriving at a conclusion"
