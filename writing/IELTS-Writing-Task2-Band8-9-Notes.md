## 1. Exam basics (from the board)

- **Essay** of **250+ words**. For Band 8–9 aim for **270–290**.
- **4 paragraphs.**
- **No contractions** (`does not`, not `doesn't`) — this is a formal essay, unlike Task 1.
- **"Any person"** — unlike Task 1, **first person is allowed** to state your opinion
  (`I believe`, `In my view`, `I would argue`). Avoid constant `I think`, and avoid second
  person `you` in general arguments.
- **Opinion** must be clear and sustained — this is a Task Response requirement.

## 2. The seven question types (syllabus on the board)

| Type | What you must do |
|---|---|
| Advantage – Disadvantage | Present both sides; usually give a judgement |
| Agree – Disagree | State your position and degree; defend it throughout |
| Positive – Negative | Assess whether the development is positive or negative, with reasons |
| Problem – Solution | Identify problem(s) **and** give matching solution(s) |
| Cause – Effect | Explain causes and consequences |
| Discussion type | Discuss both views **and** give your own opinion |
| Double type | Answer **both** questions fully — a common cap at Band 5 if one is missed |

## 3. Benefit / drawback vocabulary (from the board)

| Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|
| benefit | drawback |
| merit | demerit |
| upside | downside |
| pros | cons |
| bright side | dark side |
| advantage | disadvantage · **detrimental** · **adverse** |

Use these as **collocations**, not interchangeable labels: `a significant drawback`,
`a detrimental effect on`, `an adverse consequence`.

## 4. Analysing the prompt — class example

> *Some people opine that auto rickshaws should be banned at present.*
> (i) Do you agree or disagree? · (ii) Is this a positive or negative improvement?
> (iii) Do the advantages of this trend outweigh its disadvantages?

**Do this before writing:** circle every question. One prompt can contain **several** tasks —
missing one caps Task Response at Band 5. Your thesis must answer all of them, and your
conclusion must return to them.

## 5. The 4-paragraph structure + P-E-E-R body paragraphs

| Paragraph | Job |
|---|---|
| **1. Introduction** | Paraphrase the topic + thesis answering **all** parts of the question |
| **2. Body 1** | One main idea: **Point → Explanation → Example → Result** |
| **3. Body 2** | Opposing/main second idea, same P-E-E-R pattern |
| **4. Conclusion** | Answer the question again; no new ideas |

**Body openers from your teacher:**
- *On the one hand, there are clear benefits of using auto rickshaws.*
- *On the other hand, there are numerous drawbacks of using auto rickshaws.*

**Point connectors:** `primarily` · `firstly` · `moreover` · `furthermore` · `additionally` · `in addition`
**Result connectors:** `as a result` · `as a consequence` · `consequently` · `hence` · `thus` · `thereby` · `therefore`

⚠️ Use **2–3** result linkers, not four in every paragraph — Band 8 cohesion should be
almost invisible. Prefer referencing (`this`, `such a risk`, `these costs`).

## 6. Full Band 8–9 model essay (auto rickshaw prompt)

Auto rickshaws — three-wheeled motorised vehicles — are a common form of public transport in
Bangladesh, but their growing numbers have prompted calls for a ban. While they offer
affordable, rapid short-distance travel, I argue that the safety and environmental costs
outweigh these benefits, and that a complete ban is justified unless proper regulation is
introduced.

On the one hand, there are clear benefits to using auto rickshaws. They are extremely fast and
manoeuvrable in congested streets: a paddle rickshaw may take roughly an hour to travel from
Banani to Mohakhali, whereas an auto rickshaw can make the same journey in about twenty
minutes. This saves passengers a considerable amount of time. In addition, fares are relatively
low, so people on modest incomes can afford door-to-door transport that would otherwise be
unavailable to them. For these reasons, many commuters regard the vehicles as a practical
solution to urban mobility problems.

On the other hand, the drawbacks are more serious. Primarily, auto rickshaws are involved in a
disproportionately high number of road accidents, largely because many drivers lack formal
training and vehicles are poorly maintained. Furthermore, their engines consume substantial
quantities of fuel and emit heavy pollutants, which worsens air quality in already congested
cities. Moreover, the noise and emissions affect pedestrians and cyclists, who bear the costs
without receiving any of the benefits. These problems affect the whole community, whereas the
advantages mainly benefit individual passengers.

In conclusion, while auto rickshaws are undeniably convenient and affordable, the safety risks
and environmental damage they cause are too severe to ignore. I firmly believe that they should
be removed from city streets unless a rigorous licensing and emissions regime can be enforced.
Ultimately, the outcome depends on whether the authorities are willing to regulate rather than
simply prohibit.

*(≈275 words)*

**Why it is Band 8–9**
- **TR:** all three prompt questions answered — agreement (qualified), negative judgement, costs outweigh benefits.
- **CC:** clear progression; paragraphing with one central topic; referencing instead of stacked linkers.
- **LR:** `manoeuvrable`, `disproportionately`, `two-stroke` avoided, `emissions`, `rigorous licensing regime` — precise and collocation-correct.
- **GRA:** complex structures used accurately — concessives, relative clauses, `whereas`, conditionals; majority error-free.

## 7. Corrections to the notebook version

| Notebook wording | Issue | Band 8–9 version |
|---|---|---|
| `Some people opine that...` | `opine` is archaic/over-formal | `Some people argue / believe that...` |
| `banned at present` | `at present` is redundant | `should be banned in cities` |
| `Auto rickshaw is three wheeler motorized vehicle` | Article + plural + hyphen | `Auto rickshaws are three-wheeled motorised vehicles` |
| `which is a popular form` | Agreement | `which are a popular form` |
| `must be prohibited` | Over-strong modal | `should be banned` |
| `it causes a lot of problems` (dash before it) | Vague + wrong punctuation | `they pose serious safety risks` |
| `wastes electricity` | **Factually wrong** — rickshaws run on fuel/CNG | `consume substantial quantities of fuel` |
| `Therefore, I believe it should be banned` twice | Repetition | vary: `I firmly believe they should be removed from city streets` |
| `according to my perspective` | Awkward | `from my perspective` / omit |
| `it should be taken off the streets` | Agreement | `they should be taken off the streets` |
| `benefits using autorickshaw` | Missing preposition | `benefits of using auto rickshaws` |
| `I will elaborate on this viewpoint and provide a reasoned conclusion` | **Meta-commentary — do not write this in the exam** | Just state the thesis and develop it |
| `it will depend upon the mindset of individuals that which view they are in favour of` | Garbled | `the outcome depends on whether the authorities are willing to regulate` |

## 8. Common lines — use with care

| Board line | Verdict |
|---|---|
| *Although X is convenient and fast, it causes accidents. Therefore, I believe it should be banned.* | Good thesis shape — keep, but add precision and degree |
| *I will elaborate on this viewpoint and provide a reasoned conclusion.* | ❌ Never write this — it is a comment about your essay, not content |
| *However, it will ultimately depend upon the mindset of individuals...* | Rewrite as a clear final judgement |

## 9. Teacher's quantitative checklist — how to read it

The board lists targets: `2× topic sentence · 2× adjective/adverb · 4× point · 4× adj/adv ·
4× result · 4× connector · 3× explanation · 1× example`; conclusion: `compound + 2× complex +
4× linker + 4× vocabulary`.

**Use this as a development scaffold, not a rule.** Band 8–9 rewards *quality and relevance*,
not hitting quotas. Four result linkers in one body paragraph looks mechanical — replace two of
them with referencing. One specific example that is fully explained is worth three vague ones.
