---

## 1. Exam basics (from the board)

- **Essay** of **250+ words**. For Band 8–9 aim for **270–290**.
- **4 paragraphs.**
- **No contractions** (`does not`, not `doesn't`) — this is a formal essay, unlike Task 1.
- **"Any person"** — unlike Task 1, **first person is allowed** to state your opinion
  (`I believe`, `In my view`, `I would argue`). Avoid constant `I think`, and avoid second
  person `you` in general arguments.
- **Opinion** must be clear and sustained — this is a Task Response requirement.

## 2. The seven question types (syllabus on the board)

| Type | What you must do |
|---|---|
| Advantage – Disadvantage | Present both sides; usually give a judgement |
| Agree – Disagree | State your position and degree; defend it throughout |
| Positive – Negative | Assess whether the development is positive or negative, with reasons |
| Problem – Solution | Identify problem(s) **and** give matching solution(s) |
| Cause – Effect | Explain causes and consequences |
| Discussion type | Discuss both views **and** give your own opinion |
| Double type | Answer **both** questions fully — a common cap at Band 5 if one is missed |

## 3. Benefit / drawback vocabulary (from the board)

| Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|
| benefit | drawback |
| merit | demerit |
| upside | downside |
| pros | cons |
| bright side | dark side |
| advantage | disadvantage · **detrimental** · **adverse** |

Use these as **collocations**, not interchangeable labels: `a significant drawback`,
`a detrimental effect on`, `an adverse consequence`.

## 4. Analysing the prompt — class example

> *Some people opine that auto rickshaws should be banned at present.*
> (i) Do you agree or disagree? · (ii) Is this a positive or negative improvement?
> (iii) Do the advantages of this trend outweigh its disadvantages?

**Do this before writing:** circle every question. One prompt can contain **several** tasks —
missing one caps Task Response at Band 5. Your thesis must answer all of them, and your
conclusion must return to them.

## 5. The 4-paragraph structure + P-E-E-R body paragraphs

| Paragraph | Job |
|---|---|
| **1. Introduction** | Paraphrase the topic + thesis answering **all** parts of the question |
| **2. Body 1** | One main idea: **Point → Explanation → Example → Result** |
| **3. Body 2** | Opposing/main second idea, same P-E-E-R pattern |
| **4. Conclusion** | Answer the question again; no new ideas |

**Body openers from your teacher:**
- *On the one hand, there are clear benefits of using auto rickshaws.*
- *On the other hand, there are numerous drawbacks of using auto rickshaws.*

**Point connectors:** `primarily` · `firstly` · `moreover` · `furthermore` · `additionally` · `in addition`
**Result connectors:** `as a result` · `as a consequence` · `consequently` · `hence` · `thus` · `thereby` · `therefore`

⚠️ Use **2–3** result linkers, not four in every paragraph — Band 8 cohesion should be
almost invisible. Prefer referencing (`this`, `such a risk`, `these costs`).

## 6. Full Band 8–9 model essay (auto rickshaw prompt)

Auto rickshaws — three-wheeled motorised vehicles — are a common form of public transport in
Bangladesh, but their growing numbers have prompted calls for a ban. While they offer
affordable, rapid short-distance travel, I argue that the safety and environmental costs
outweigh these benefits, and that a complete ban is justified unless proper regulation is
introduced.

On the one hand, there are clear benefits to using auto rickshaws. They are extremely fast and
manoeuvrable in congested streets: a paddle rickshaw may take roughly an hour to travel from
Banani to Mohakhali, whereas an auto rickshaw can make the same journey in about twenty
minutes. This saves passengers a considerable amount of time. In addition, fares are relatively
low, so people on modest incomes can afford door-to-door transport that would otherwise be
unavailable to them. For these reasons, many commuters regard the vehicles as a practical
solution to urban mobility problems.

On the other hand, the drawbacks are more serious. Primarily, auto rickshaws are involved in a
disproportionately high number of road accidents, largely because many drivers lack formal
training and vehicles are poorly maintained. Furthermore, their engines consume substantial
quantities of fuel and emit heavy pollutants, which worsens air quality in already congested
cities. Moreover, the noise and emissions affect pedestrians and cyclists, who bear the costs
without receiving any of the benefits. These problems affect the whole community, whereas the
advantages mainly benefit individual passengers.

In conclusion, while auto rickshaws are undeniably convenient and affordable, the safety risks
and environmental damage they cause are too severe to ignore. I firmly believe that they should
be removed from city streets unless a rigorous licensing and emissions regime can be enforced.
Ultimately, the outcome depends on whether the authorities are willing to regulate rather than
simply prohibit.

*(≈275 words)*

**Why it is Band 8–9**
- **TR:** all three prompt questions answered — agreement (qualified), negative judgement, costs outweigh benefits.
- **CC:** clear progression; paragraphing with one central topic; referencing instead of stacked linkers.
- **LR:** `manoeuvrable`, `disproportionately`, `two-stroke` avoided, `emissions`, `rigorous licensing regime` — precise and collocation-correct.
- **GRA:** complex structures used accurately — concessives, relative clauses, `whereas`, conditionals; majority error-free.

## 8. Common lines — use with care

| Board line | Verdict |
|---|---|
| *Although X is convenient and fast, it causes accidents. Therefore, I believe it should be banned.* | Good thesis shape — keep, but add precision and degree |
| *I will elaborate on this viewpoint and provide a reasoned conclusion.* | ❌ Never write this — it is a comment about your essay, not content |
| *However, it will ultimately depend upon the mindset of individuals...* | Rewrite as a clear final judgement |

## 9. Teacher's quantitative checklist — how to read it

The board lists targets: `2× topic sentence · 2× adjective/adverb · 4× point · 4× adj/adv ·
4× result · 4× connector · 3× explanation · 1× example`; conclusion: `compound + 2× complex +
4× linker + 4× vocabulary`.

**Use this as a development scaffold, not a rule.** Band 8–9 rewards *quality and relevance*,
not hitting quotas. Four result linkers in one body paragraph looks mechanical — replace two of
them with referencing. One specific example that is fully explained is worth three vague ones.

## 10. Band 8–9 checklist for Task 2

- [ ] Every part of the prompt answered in the thesis and again in the conclusion
- [ ] Position clear, consistent, and qualified where appropriate (`largely`, `to some extent`)
- [ ] Two body paragraphs, each with one main idea developed P-E-E-R
- [ ] Ideas supported with relevant, specific examples — no over-generalisation
- [ ] Topic-specific collocations used accurately
- [ ] Majority of sentences error-free; no repeated systematic errors
- [ ] Cohesion unobtrusive — referencing over linkers
- [ ] 270–290 words, 4 paragraphs, no contractions
- [ ] Conclusion gives a final judgement, not a new idea

## 1. What class 2 reinforced

- **Advantage vs disadvantage / outweigh prompts** — answer the exact question: *"Do the
  advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"* → give an explicit verdict.
- **Positive or negative development** → state clearly which one, and keep it consistent.
- Thesis pattern: *Although X is [benefit], Y is [drawback]. Therefore, from my perspective, [verdict].*
- Body 1: `On the one hand, there are clear benefits...`
- Body 2: `On the other hand, significant drawbacks remain...`
- Support: `Firstly` · `Furthermore` · `To cite an example` · `As a result` · `Consequently`
- Conclusion: `In conclusion, while..., ...` → `Hence, I firmly believe that...`

⚠️ **Exam rule:** never write *"I will elaborate on this viewpoint and provide a reasoned
conclusion"* or *"before arriving at a justified final thought"*. Those are comments about
your essay, not content. Your teacher uses them as classroom scaffolding — leave them out.

## 2. Essay A — watching live performances at home

**Prompt**
> *These days, many people like to watch live performances (such as concerts and shows) at
> home, either on TV or on a computer, instead of attending in person. Do the advantages of
> this trend outweigh its disadvantages?*

### Band 8–9 model answer

These days, many people prefer watching concerts and shows on television or on a computer
rather than attending in person. While this option is undeniably convenient and far cheaper,
I argue that the loss of atmosphere and the poorer quality of the experience outweigh these
benefits.

On the one hand, there are clear benefits to watching live performances from home. Firstly,
it is incredibly convenient: viewers do not have to travel long distances, queue for entry or
arrange transport home, so they can remain in their own living room and save a considerable
amount of time. Furthermore, digital viewing is far more affordable. Tickets for major shows
are often prohibitively expensive, whereas streaming allows anyone to enjoy the arts without
spending a fortune. As a result, people can save money on tickets, transport and food while
still accessing world-class performances from their own devices.

On the other hand, significant drawbacks remain. First and foremost, the key loss is the
absence of a genuine crowd atmosphere. At a live show, the energy exchanged between performers
and audience creates a unique experience that cannot be replicated on a screen, resulting in a
weaker emotional connection. Additionally, the limited sound quality of small speakers cannot
compare with the powerful audio systems and light displays of a real venue. For instance, a
rock concert in a stadium offers high-fidelity sound and dazzling lighting that engage both
the ears and the whole body. Watching the same event on a laptop reduces it to a diluted
version of the artist's original intention.

In conclusion, while watching from home is comfortable and economical, the lack of genuine
atmosphere and the inferior sensory experience make it a lesser experience. I firmly believe
that being present in person remains essential, and that the merits of home viewing do not
outweigh the drawbacks. Ultimately, the preference depends on the individual's priorities.

*(≈275 words)*

## 3. Essay B — fast-food sponsorship in schools

**Prompt**
> *Nowadays, marketing has left no stone unturned to entice customers. Certainly, fast food
> companies and restaurants now tie up with schools to endorse their products. Do you think
> this is a positive or negative development?*

### Band 8–9 model answer

Nowadays, marketing has left no stone unturned in its effort to entice customers, and
fast-food companies increasingly partner with schools to promote their products. Although
such deals provide schools with additional funding and relieve logistical pressure, I argue
that they harm children's health and undermine the educational mission of schools. This is
therefore a negative development.

On the one hand, some people argue that these partnerships are clearly beneficial. Firstly,
financial contributions from food giants can be used to fund extracurricular activities or
upgrade classroom technology. For example, institutions can offer scholarships to students in
need using this money. Consequently, schools with limited budgets can provide resources that
would otherwise be unaffordable. Furthermore, several fast-food companies organise catering
services that reduce the logistical burden on school administrators. The school can then focus
more on academic management while ensuring a consistent food supply for the student body.

On the other hand, multiple drawbacks are associated with this trend. First and foremost,
children need access to healthy food in order to grow and learn effectively. Studies indicate
that students who consume excessive sugar and fat struggle to concentrate in class, so
introducing junk food into schools contradicts children's nutritional needs. Furthermore,
daily exposure to these brands can instil poor eating habits and encourage impulsive purchasing
decisions. Consequently, such behaviour may lead to health problems such as obesity and
diabetes later in life.

In conclusion, while the additional revenue and logistical support are undoubtedly appealing
to schools, the detrimental effects on students' health are far more consequential. I firmly
believe this is a negative development. Schools should focus on maintaining a proper learning
environment and the wellbeing of children rather than providing platforms for corporations to
maximise their profits.

*(≈265 words)*

## 1. The new lesson: Discussion type

For **"Discuss both views and give your opinion"**, the structure is different from an opinion essay:

| Paragraph | Rule |
|---|---|
| **1. Introduction** | Paraphrase **both** views. **No opinion here.** |
| **2. Body 1** | View 1 — topic sentence + Point → Explanation → Example → Result |
| **3. Body 2** | View 2 — same structure |
| **4. Conclusion** | Conclusion word → your opinion → `view 1 → view 2 → view 1` → `not only... but also` / `as well as` → suggestion |

Useful twist words for linking the two views: `that` · `however` · `whereas`.

⚠️ Avoid meta-commentary such as *"an attempt will be made to examine both perspectives"* or
*"this viewpoint will be further explained to support a well-founded conclusion"*. A brief
*"This essay will examine both views before presenting my opinion"* is acceptable, but simply
paraphrasing the two views is stronger and more natural.

## 2. Essay A — Nuclear energy

**Prompt**
> *Nuclear energy is better than other energy resources in meeting the ever-increasing needs
> of the globe. To what extent do you agree or disagree?*

### Band 8–9 model answer

Nuclear energy is often presented as a sustainable and comparatively clean solution to the
world's growing energy needs. While it avoids the depletion of fossil fuels and emits little
carbon dioxide, I argue that its high costs and serious safety risks mean it is not superior
to other energy sources.

On the one hand, I understand why many people advocate building more nuclear power stations.
Primarily, nuclear power generates electricity without depleting scarce non-renewable resources
such as coal, oil and gas, which are becoming increasingly limited. As a result, it is widely
regarded as a promising option for conserving these resources for future generations.
Furthermore, nuclear plants emit far less carbon dioxide and fewer toxic substances than
coal-fired plants. By using nuclear resources, countries can reduce the emissions that drive
global warming.

On the contrary, nuclear energy carries significant dangers. First and foremost, the primary
concern is the potential for a radiation accident with long-lasting detrimental effects on
both people and the environment. A notable example is the Fukushima disaster of 2011, which
forced the evacuation of thousands of people from their homes. The possibility of such a
catastrophe makes nuclear power a poor choice for the future. Additionally, construction is
extremely expensive: a single plant can cost billions of dollars and places a heavy financial
burden on governments.

In conclusion, while nuclear power is both powerful and comparatively clean, the associated
risks and high costs are considerable. I firmly believe that governments should prioritise
safer and more affordable alternatives such as wind and solar power. Ultimately, however, the
choice comes down to which risks a society is willing to accept.

*(≈270 words)*

## 3. Essay B — Driving age (Discussion type)

**Prompt**
> *Most countries allow 18-year-olds to start driving a car. Some say it is good to allow
> driving at that age. Others think that the age to start driving should be at least 25 years.
> Discuss both views and give your opinion.*

### Band 8–9 model answer

In most countries, eighteen-year-olds are permitted to drive, but some people believe the
minimum age should be raised to 25. There are strong arguments on both sides of this debate.

On the one hand, numerous people support allowing 18-year-olds to drive. Firstly, it gives
young adults a valuable sense of independence: they can commute to university or work without
relying on their parents, and this helps them become self-reliant. Moreover, driving opens up
better employment opportunities and enables young people to support their families. In rural
areas, where public transport such as buses and trains is limited, a car can be essential for
daily life. Hence, being able to drive at 18 allows young people to explore a wider range of
opportunities.

On the other hand, some believe that 25 is a more appropriate age for obtaining a licence.
Primarily, older individuals tend to be more mature and remain calmer in traffic, which
reduces the likelihood of fatal accidents. Furthermore, many collisions are caused by speeding
and distraction — problems that are more common among younger drivers. Raising the legal
driving age to 25 could therefore result in fewer road fatalities. Consequently, governments
would spend less on healthcare, and young people would be less likely to face a tragedy early
in life.

In conclusion, while raising the driving age would undoubtedly improve road safety and reduce
accidents, the freedom to travel at 18 and the opportunities it creates are also important.
Provided that strict testing and training are in place, I believe 18 remains the right age to
start driving, as it enables young adults to become responsible drivers.

*(≈265 words)*

# PART 4 — LECTURE-BACKED TASK 2 OPERATING SYSTEM

> Synthesised from Mentors Lectures 4–5. This is the execution layer on top of your class models.

## The five task types (Lecture 4)

| Type | Question ending | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Argument | *To what extent do you agree or disagree?* | Clear position; persuade with reasons |
| Cause and effect | *Why? What effect...?* | Causes + effects; no solutions unless asked |
| Discussion | *Discuss both views and give your opinion* | Both views in separate paragraphs + your opinion |
| Advantages/disadvantages | *What are the advantages and disadvantages?* | Both sides, no opinion needed |
| Double question | *Why? Is this positive or negative?* | **Both** questions, equal weight |

Same topic → different tasks. Decode the question stem before writing.

## Thesis statement rules (Lecture 4)

- One sentence at the **end of the introduction**; says what the essay is about, which points form
  the body, and your position.
- **No rhetorical questions.** Be brief and decisive. Give reasons where possible.
- **Never write an outline sentence** — *"This essay would like to explore reasons for this in
  more detail"* is clichéd and memorised; examiners are trained to spot it.
- Good model (corrected): *As rickshaws are slow, are a major cause of traffic congestion, and
  cannot meet long-distance travel needs, I believe they should be banned from Dhaka City.*

## Counter-argument (Lectures 4–5)

Acknowledge the opposing view, then refute it — typically with `although` / `however`. This shows
you evaluated both sides and makes your verdict stronger, not weaker. One counter paragraph is
enough in a 4-paragraph essay.

## Three paraphrasing techniques (Lecture 4)

1. **Synonyms** — watch strength: `interesting` ≠ `fascinating`.
2. **Word forms** — *Facebook is an **invasion** of privacy* → *Facebook has **invaded** our privacy*.
3. **Clause reordering** — *It is difficult to say whether the economy will improve* →
   *Whether the economy will improve is difficult to say.*

Copied words from the prompt do **not** count towards the word count.

## Cause/effect and double-question rules (Lecture 5)

- Causes/effects: discuss causes and effects; **do not add solutions** unless asked.
- Causes/solutions: discuss causes and solutions; **omit effects**.
- Double question: both questions need **equal importance and space** — missing one caps Task
  Response at Band 5.

## Final 5-minute editing checklist (Lecture 5)

1. Consistent tenses and subject–verb agreement.
2. Word formation; `a/an`, `the`, prepositions.
3. Range of sentence structures.
4. Appropriate vocabulary.
5. Accurate spelling.
6. Topic sentence for each paragraph; all points related to it.
7. Connectives used effectively and in the right place.
8. Each paragraph adequately developed.
9. Enough detail and sufficient examples/facts.
10. A definite point of view developed.
11. Every paragraph helps address the task.

**Band 5 diagnostic from the handout:** ideas can be present and still score only 5.0 if the third
paragraph is short and confusing, linkers are misplaced (`However`, `In addition`), referencing is
loose (`they`, `it`), vocabulary is basic and grammar has repeated article errors (*a important*).

## Language bank (Lecture 5)
