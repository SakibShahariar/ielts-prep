## 1. Line graphs

**Handout definition:** a chart displaying information as a series of data points connected by
straight line segments; used to visualise a **trend over intervals of time** (a time series),
so the line is drawn chronologically.

**Sample charts in the handout**
- *Australia Student Enrollment, 2021* — roughly 40,000 in January, a March peak near 60,000, a
  May low around 35,000, a gradual rise to ~50,000 by August–October, a second peak near 62,000
  in November, ending around 55,000.
- *Unemployment Percentages, 2009–2016* — USA falls from about 7.8% to 4.5%; Japan rises from
  about 3% to a 2014 peak near 5.6%, then falls to ~4.6%.
- *Twitter use by age group, USA 2015–2022* — 18–29 rises from ~14% to ~52%; 30–49 from ~8% to
  ~31%; 50–64 from ~7% to ~15%.
- *Sales of DVDs* — Previous ~200, January 500, February ~400, March ~1,100, April ~900, May ~900.

⚠️ The handout prose says February 300, March 1,000 and April 800 — those do **not** match its own
chart. Always read the chart, not the sample text.

## 2. Describing data vs describing trends

**Describing data** = identifying numerical facts and stating them accurately. The handout's
DVD example: *"Sales before January were only 200 and rose to 500 in January; they fell in
February, shot up in March, fell again in April and remained steady in May."*

**Describing trends** is harder. A **trend** is something that keeps occurring over a period:
a rise over three consecutive weeks, a decline over four successive months. A trend needs
**more than one period** — more than a month, week or day. So an up-down-up-down sequence over
January–April is not one overall trend.

**Sample: Sales of Mobile Phones** (Previous ~1,000 → Jan 1,500 → Feb 2,500 → Mar 3,000 peak →
Apr 2,000 → May 1,500 → Jun 700): an **increasing trend from December to March**, followed by a
**declining trend from April to June** — because each movement lasts several successive periods.

> **Band 8–9 rule:** name the sustained direction in the overview; keep individual rises and
> falls for the body paragraphs.

## 3. Handout sample response — unemployment, USA & Japan (2015–2021)

The handout gives an ~300-word model. Condensed to Band 8–9 length (165–185 words):

> The line graph compares unemployment rates in the United States and Japan between March 2015
> and March 2021.
>
> Overall, unemployment declined in the US but increased in Japan, so the gap between the two
> countries narrowed and both finished at roughly 5%.
>
> In March 2015, the American rate stood at about 7%, considerably above Japan's 2.5%. Over the
> following years it followed a broadly downward path despite minor fluctuations, falling to just
> over 5% by 2018 and remaining relatively stable thereafter. A slight dip in 2020 was followed
> by a marginal recovery to roughly 5% at the end of the period.
>
> Japan, by contrast, witnessed a sustained rise. The figure climbed steadily from 2.5% in 2015
> to around 4.3% by 2018, with a brief dip that year, then resumed its upward path to peak at just
> over 5% in 2020. After a minor fall, it stabilised at approximately 5%, matching the US rate.

⚠️ The original handout response is labelled "approximately 300 words". That is too long for a
20-minute Task 1 — aim for 165–185.

## 4. Tables

**Handout guidance:**
- Data are **precisely provided** — you interpret and compare them; no need to hunt for figures.
- Comparative descriptions are almost always required.
- **Do not describe all the data.** Focus on the most significant figures first so you don't miss them.
- Look for the highest, lowest and other significant data; **group** the information.

## 5. Language of incorporating data

**Two sentence forms** (same meaning, different grammar):

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| Noun form | *There was a rapid increase in the number of computers sold.* |
| Verb form | *The number of computers sold increased rapidly.* |

**Pattern:** `Subject + verb of change + adverb of amount`
- Sales increased slightly · Sales increased suddenly · Sales decreased moderately

**Alternative with `there`:**
`From January to February, there was a slight increase in sales.`

| Time period | `there` | Adjective | Noun of change | Subject |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| From January to February | there was a | slight | increase | in sales |

**Another pattern:** `... is / has been / will be + a + adjective + noun + in...`
Adjectives: `slight · slow · moderate · gradual · dramatic`; nouns: `increase · growth · fall ·
drop · decrease · decline`.

**Two ways to incorporate a figure:**
1. **Brackets:** *In January the number of ceiling fans sold (60) was greater than the number sold (30) in February.*
2. **Relative clause:** *...the number of ceiling fans sold, which accounts for 60, was greater than the number sold, which accounts for only 30, in February.*

⚠️ The handout's phrase "accounts for 60" is incomplete — write `accounts for 60 units / 60%`.

## 6. Prepositions for data (`from`, `to`, `by`, `of`, `at`, `with`)

| Meaning | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Starting and ending point | `from... to...` | It decreased **from** 10 **to** 2. |
| Starting point / peak | `at` / `with` | It started **at/with** 2. It peaked **at** 10. |
| Size of change | `by` / `of` | There was a drop **of** 8 units. It dropped **by** 8 units. |

**Exercise answers (from the handout):**
1. remained constant **at** 5 ppm · 2. increased rapidly **to** well over 550 ·
3. peak **at** 20 billion · 4. drop **by** 50% / increase **of** 55% · 5. finish the year **at**
500 units · 6. began **at** 50, rose **by** about 20, end **at** well over 600 · 7. **at** 5,000
people · 8. dropped **by** 400 **to** 576 per thousand · 9. ranging **from** 15% **to** 55% ·
10. unchanged **at** 4,500, rose **to** 555,000.

## 7. Approximation and time

**Approximation words:** nearly · almost · just about · approximately · roughly · about · around ·
just over · just under · in the region of · just below · more or less · a little more than ·
round about.

**Time complements:** after · at (time) · at last · at the turn of the century/decade · before ·
between... and... · during · earlier · every (number) years/months/days · first, second, third ·
former, latter · in (year) · in the 1980s / first half of the century · within a time span of 10
years · later · next · then · subsequently · on (day) · previous · prior to · simultaneously /
at the same time as · since... · while.

## 8. Language of comparison

| Subject of reporting | Scale (high) | Scale (low) |
|---|---|---|
| Number | greater | smaller / fewer |
| Percentage | higher | lower |
| Rate | higher | lower |
| Difference | huge | tiny / marginal |

**Degree words:** substantially · considerably · far · much · somewhat · a lot · significantly ·
slightly · fractionally · overwhelmingly · barely · hardly · merely · moderately · marginally ·
drastically.

**Sameness:** `the same as` · `equal to/with` · `identical to/with` · `X is absolutely / just /
exactly / precisely / almost / nearly / practically / more or less / about / not quite the same as Y`.

**Comparison frames:** `Compared to..., the change in... was...` · `In... and..., the proportion
of... was...` · `In both... and...` · `While the... went up substantially, the... increased slowly` ·
`The figures for... showed a...% rise, whereas those for... fell by...%`.

NB from the handout: keep the report **relevant, accurate and not repetitive**.

## 9. Mixed graph sample — Astoria crime, 2017

Two pie charts: **females 9% arrested / 91% not**; **males 28% arrested / 72% not**.
Bar chart — reasons for arrests (female / male approximate): drink driving ~16%/17%; public
drinking ~24%/30%; breach of order ~25%/30%; assault ~10%/13%; theft ~13%/14%; other reasons
~14%/13%; no answer ~7%/7%.

**Band 8–9 model overview + body (condensed from the handout's 220-word sample):**

> The two pie charts and the bar chart depict the crime situation in Astoria in 2017.
>
> Overall, men were arrested considerably more often than women, and public drinking was the
> most common reason for arrest among both genders, while no answer accounted for the smallest
> proportion in both cases.
>
> The pie charts show that while only a marginal 9% of females in Astoria were arrested, the
> figure for males was more than three times higher, at 28%. Consequently, the proportion of
> women who were not arrested was far greater than that of men.
>
> Turning to the bar chart, public drinking was the leading reason for arrest for both genders,
> at roughly 24% of females and 30% of males. By contrast, no answer was the least common
> category, at about 7% for both. The most striking gender difference appears in breach of order,
> where females outnumbered males; for assault and other reasons, the figures stood at around
> 10% for women and 13% for men.

## 11. Band 8–9 checklist from Lecture 2

- [ ] Trend = sustained movement over more than one period; name it in the overview
- [ ] Individual rises/falls kept for body paragraphs
- [ ] Tables: select the significant figures, group related data, do not list everything
- [ ] Vary sentence form: noun form (`there was a slight increase`) and verb form (`sales increased slightly`)
- [ ] Prepositions correct: `from...to`, `at`, `by`, `of`
- [ ] Approximation and comparison vocabulary varied — not repetitive
- [ ] Mixed graphs: one visual per body paragraph
- [ ] 165–185 words in ~20 minutes
