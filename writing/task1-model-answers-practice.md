# IELTS Writing Task 1 — Practice Graphs: Band 8–9 Model Answers

> Three full model answers written to the official descriptors. Each is 165–185 words,
> 4 paragraphs, with no bullets and an impersonal register. Learn the patterns — do not memorise.
>
> **Source tags (as reported):** urban population line graph — Classes 3–4 · Tilden Airport maps — Class 4 · instant noodle process — Class 4.

## Model 1 — Line graph: urban population in four Asian countries *(Classes 3–4)*

**Task:** *The graph below gives information about the percentage of the population in four
Asian countries living in cities from 1970 to 2020, with predictions for 2030 and 2040.*

### Model answer

The line graph illustrates changes in the proportion of the population living in cities in four
Asian countries between 1970 and 2020, with projections extending to 2040.

Overall, Malaysia is expected to remain the most urbanised country throughout the period, while
Indonesia is forecast to overtake the Philippines after 2020. All four nations are predicted to
see further growth by 2040.

Looking at the historical figures, Malaysia rose steadily from about 30% in 1970 to roughly 75%
in 2020. The Philippines followed a fluctuating path, peaking at around 48% in 1990 before
dipping to approximately 42% in 2010 and recovering to 45% a decade later. Indonesia, meanwhile,
started lowest at just 14%, but climbed consistently to reach 52% over the same period.

As for the projections, Malaysia's share is likely to level off near 83% by 2040, whereas
Indonesia's is set to rise sharply to about 64%, overtaking the Philippines. The latter is
forecast to recover to roughly 56%. Thailand, by contrast, is expected to remain the least
urbanised, increasing only gradually from 33% to just under 50%.

*(≈185 words)*

### Why this is Band 8–9
- **TA:** all four countries covered, actual and predicted periods separated, key features selected (Malaysia's lead, Indonesia's overtake, Philippines' fluctuation, Thailand's lag).
- **CC:** progression from overview → historical → projected; `whereas`, `by contrast`, `meanwhile`, `the latter` used precisely.
- **LR:** `urbanised`, `projections`, `fluctuating path`, `peaking`, `levelling off`, `overtaking` — precise and natural.
- **GRA:** wide range — `is expected to`, `is forecast to`, `before dipping`, `whereas`, `by contrast`; majority error-free.
- **Key data:** Malaysia 30→75→83 · Philippines 32→48→42→45→56 · Indonesia 14→52→64 · Thailand 19→33→49.

## Model 2 — Map task: Tilden Airport 1985 vs 2015 *(Class 4)*

**Task:** *The two maps show Tilden Airport and the surrounding area in 1985 and 2015. Write a
report for a university lecturer summarising the information shown in the maps.*

### Model answer

The two maps illustrate how Tilden Airport and its surrounding area changed between 1985 and 2015.

Overall, the site underwent substantial redevelopment: the airport was renamed, a second runway
was constructed, and former agricultural and industrial land was converted to commercial and
residential use.

Looking at the airport itself, the international terminal was expanded considerably, while the
domestic terminal and car park remained broadly unchanged. A new diagonal runway, Runway Two,
was built across the northern part of the site, and a bus and light rail station was constructed
beside the car park, linked by a light rail line running east along the main road. Airport
Support Services were relocated further east and enlarged.

Turning to the surrounding area, Johnson's Farm was replaced by a business park to the north and
a housing estate in the north-east corner. The factories on the southern side were demolished;
in their place, a hotel and conference centre was built alongside the enlarged Airport Support
Services. The road to the City of Tilden, eight kilometres east, remained in the same position.

*(≈180 words)*

### Why this is Band 8–9
- **TA:** all major changes covered — terminals, runway, transport, support services, farm, factories, road.
- **CC:** clear grouping — airport itself vs surrounding area; `while`, `beside`, `in their place`, `alongside`.
- **LR:** `underwent substantial redevelopment`, `relocated`, `demolished`, `converted`, `in the north-east corner`.
- **GRA:** past simple passive throughout — `was renamed`, `was constructed`, `was expanded`, `were demolished`, `was built`.
- **Grammar note:** `Airport Support Services **were** relocated` (plural subject) — a common agreement trap.

## Model 3 — Process diagram: instant noodles *(Class 4)*

**Task:** *The diagram below shows how instant noodles are manufactured.*

### Model answer

The diagram illustrates the eight stages involved in the manufacture of instant noodles.

Overall, the process begins with the delivery of raw materials to storage silos and culminates in
labelling and sealing, and the entire operation is fully mechanised.

At the first stage, flour is transported by truck to two storage silos. It is then fed into a
mixer, where water and oil are added, and the mixture passes through rollers to produce dough
sheets. These sheets are subsequently cut into thin dough strips, which are then formed into
noodle discs.

In the following stage, the discs are cooked in oil and dried before vegetables and spices are
added as they are placed into cups. Finally, each cup is labelled and sealed, completing the
packaging stage before the finished product is distributed.

*(≈165 words)*

### Why this is Band 8–9
- **TA:** all eight stages covered in chronological order; overview states number of stages plus first and last.
- **CC:** `At the first stage`, `then`, `subsequently`, `In the following stage`, `Finally` — clear sequencing without mechanical `firstly/secondly`.
- **LR:** `culminates in`, `fully mechanised`, `fed into`, `produce dough sheets`, `formed into`, `packaging stage`.
- **GRA:** present simple passive throughout — `is transported`, `is fed`, `are added`, `are cooked`, `is labelled and sealed`.
- **Trap avoided:** `equipment` is uncountable — never `equipments`.

## Quick comparison of the three task types

| Task type | Overview focus | Main tense | Typical linkers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Line graph** | Highest/lowest, biggest change, crossing points, predictions | Past simple + future projections | `whereas` · `by contrast` · `meanwhile` |
| **Map** | Overall transformation, new/removed/replaced features | **Past simple passive** | `while` · `in their place` · `alongside` |
| **Process** | Number of stages, first and last stage | **Present simple passive** | `then` · `subsequently` · `finally` |
