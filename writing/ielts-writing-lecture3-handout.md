## 1. Writing the body and "conclusion"

**Identification of main features** (handout):
- You do **not** need to describe everything. Trying to write "everything" takes too much time,
  leaves less time for Task 2, and creates more chances of mistakes in spelling, vocabulary and
  grammar — all of which lower the score.
- Select the main features. Data may show differences or changes over time, between places or
  between groups of people. Identify significant contrasts, relevant comparisons, similarities
  and trends.
- Ask **"What is the fact?"** — you are expected to **Report or Exhibit, NOT to justify**.
  Unnecessary and irrelevant material lowers the standard and loses marks for irrelevance.
  Stay with what the graph says; outside information only impairs the report.

**How to write the main part:**
- Divide the text into **3–4 paragraphs, including the introduction** (handout wording).
- Divide the information into broad/general groups, categories or trends.
- Describe the main or most **striking / significant / noticeable / outstanding / remarkable**
  features, characteristics, differences, trends or changes.
- Avoid lists of detail. Write about general trends and support them with specific data.
- Describe the general trend: `is/was upwards / downwards / flat` or say what happened
  (`sales rose / fell / remained flat / fluctuated`).
- Introduce details with: `Turning to the details...` · `As can be seen from the graph...` ·
  `At the outset it is clear...` · `At first glance it is clear...`

**"Conclusion" phrases in the handout:** `Generally,...` · `Generally speaking,...` ·
`All in all,...` · `On average,...`

> ⚠️ **Band 8–9 correction:** IELTS officially calls this paragraph an **overview**, not a
> conclusion. It presents the big picture **without exact figures** and is usually placed
> straight after the introduction, so it is not confused with an essay conclusion. A single
> summarizing sentence at the end is possible, but the clear overview is what earns Band 7+.
>
> Note: the handout's "3–4 paragraphs including the introduction" is why you saw both numbers —
> the standard 4-paragraph layout (Intro · Overview · Body 1 · Body 2) sits inside that range.

## 2. Flow charts / process diagrams

**Handout rule:** the trick is to **follow the direction of the arrows**. Describe which thing
leads to what, step by step. The agent is usually not mentioned unless a particular person
performs the task.

**Sample 1 — ACME Corporation recruitment process**

```
Submit application → Initial qualifying interview
   → Meet basic qualifications → Aptitude test
        → Pass → Six-month probation → Six-month evaluation
                    → Recommended → Permanent position offered
                    → Not recommended → Not employed
        → Fail → Not employed
   → Do not meet basic qualifications → Not employed
```

**Band 8–9 overview + body (condensed from the handout):**

> The flow chart illustrates the recruitment process for a permanent position at ACME Corporation.
>
> Overall, the process consists of several stages and includes two major assessment points, with
> applicants able to exit at three different stages.
>
> The process begins when an application is submitted, after which the candidate attends an
> initial qualifying interview. Those who fail to meet the basic requirements at this stage are
> not employed, while successful applicants move on to an aptitude test. Failing this test also
> ends the process.
>
> Candidates who pass the aptitude test begin a six-month probation period, which is followed by
> a six-month evaluation. Only those who are recommended at this point are offered a permanent
> position; otherwise, they are not employed.

**Sample 2 — Soap production: past and present**

- **1,000 years ago:** animal fat was stored → plant ashes were added → the mixture was heated.
- **Nowadays:** molten fat and water at 130°C are split into fatty acid and glycerin → alkali is
  added and the ingredients are mixed → the mixture is poured into moulds and cooled → it is
  milled through rollers with perfume added → the bars are stamped and wrapped.

**Band 8–9 point:** this is a **comparative process** — use the same impersonal passive, but
organise the body by **past method vs present method** and link with `whereas` / `by contrast`.

### Language for processes (handout)

- **Intro:** `The diagram / picture / chart shows / illustrates / describes / depicts the process of...`
- **Sequencing adverbs:** `first / firstly / first of all · secondly · thirdly · then / next /
  after / following that · subsequently / subsequent to that · finally`
- **Prepositions:** `At the beginning of...` · `At the end of...`
- **Conjunctions:** `when / once / as soon as / immediately` · `before + clause/gerund` ·
  `after + clause/gerund` · `where / from where / after which`
- **Devices:** `The first / next / final step / phase / stage is / involves...` ·
  `After this step...` · `Once this stage is completed...`
- **Grammar:** present simple for processes; passive voice when something is being made
  (`the book is printed and then collated, after which it is bound`); active voice when
  describing something happening (`the moisture evaporates and condenses`); watch
  singular/plural agreement.
- **Cycle ending:** `The cycle then repeats itself / is then repeated.`

## 3. Maps

**Handout:** maps usually show **one particular area and its development over a number of years**.
You give a detailed description of the different sections and the changes over the entire period.

**Sample — Palm Grove, 1990 and 2005:** houses, a lake, a school, a hospital and a railway
station in 1990; by 2005, blocks of flats, a supermarket, a factory and a ferry had appeared,
and the road/layout had changed.

### Language for maps (handout)

- With **`in`** use past simple passive: *The stadium was constructed in 2000.*
- With **`by`** use past perfect passive: *The stadium had been constructed by 2000.*
- Vary structure by moving the time phrase to the beginning or end.
- Useful phrases: `in the centre of / next to / adjacent to` · `built / rebuilt / replaced` ·
  `situated / located` · `changed into / added / gave way to / became / made way for / converted` ·
  `saw / witnessed considerable changes / developments` · `moreover / in addition` · `while in 1990...`

**Spatial order vocabulary:** across · across from · in · in which / to which / from which · under ·
over · inside · beside · on top of · along · through · as far as · northern / southern / eastern /
western · to the left/right-hand side · to the north · in the back of / behind · in front of ·
in the middle · adjacent · midpoint · halfway · interior · diagonal · edge · limit · parallel to ·
perpendicular to · opposite · overlapping · extension · intersection · rectangle · square ·
circle · vertical · horizontal.

**Band 8–9 note:** group changes by area (north / centre / south) instead of listing every
building; use `whereas` and `while` for before/after contrasts.

## 4. Differences between the Task 1 types (handout checklist)

**Think first**
- *Pie charts / graphs / tables:* highlight key words, note headings, dates and measurements;
  select the most important trends; choose the best examples and summarise.
- *Process diagrams / maps:* highlight key words from the task description; note all labels and
  the order of steps; describe from start to end; leave nothing out.

**Ask questions**
- *Charts:* What is the purpose? What changed over time? What are the significant trends? What is
  the most interesting feature?
- *Processes:* What is the purpose? How does it work? Are there alternative steps? What is the end result?

**Then write**
- *Introduction:* paraphrase the task description in 1–2 sentences; do not copy. For processes,
  include the purpose or end of the process.
- *Body (charts):* focus on trends and interesting points; describe the most significant data
  first; give examples to support trends; use statistics accurately.
- *Body (processes/maps):* follow each step in sequence; include every step; expand key ideas into
  sentences; use connective words to link steps.
- *Summary paragraph:* does not have to be a separate paragraph in this handout's view — a single
  summarizing sentence can round off the report. (Use the clear **overview** for Band 7+.)

## 5. Practice exercises — Band 8–9 overviews

**Pittover Library, 1984 vs today:** in 1984 the librarian's desk, children's area, newspapers,
three study areas, fiction shelves, copier, community room and non-fiction shelves; today a
building extension houses fiction/non-fiction books, a book drop-off, self-service desk, study
area with tables, multimedia centre (PCs, tablets, printer, copier) and DVDs/CDs.

> Overall, the library was substantially extended and reorganised: a multimedia centre and
> self-service facilities were introduced, while the original study areas were replaced by a
> single larger study area.

**Zain, 1990 vs today:** in 1990 a transport depot, homes, a park, factories, blocks of flats,
a café and waterfront; today the depot has become a community centre, restaurants and tables
have appeared, a ferry service has been added, and the park and waterfront have been redeveloped.

> Overall, the area changed from an industrial waterfront into a leisure and community district,
> with the transport depot replaced by a community centre and a ferry service introduced.

**Chocolate — process + price pie:** cocoa is ground (waste removed), cocoa liquor is produced;
one branch goes to industrial chocolate with sugar and other ingredients, then chocolate bars;
another branch is pressed into cocoa butter (to industrial chocolate) and cocoa powder (to the
food industry). Price split: ingredients/overheads 37% · supermarkets 34% · tax 15% · chocolate
company 10% · farmers 4%.

> Overall, the process has two main branches after the cocoa liquor stage, and the largest share
> of the price goes to ingredients and overheads rather than to farmers.

**East Africa — rainfall and temperature:** rainfall peaks sharply in April (~275mm), with a
second wetter period in October–November (~145–150mm) and a very dry June–August; temperature
peaks in June (~22°C) and is lowest in March and September (~19.3°C).

> Overall, rainfall is highly seasonal, peaking in April and falling to almost nothing between
> June and August, while temperature varies far less, reaching its highest point in June.

## 6. Band 8–9 checklist from Lecture 3

- [ ] Report the facts — do not justify or add outside information
- [ ] Select main features; avoid lists of detail
- [ ] Overview present and clear, with no exact figures
- [ ] Processes: follow arrows, present passive, every step included, no agent unless needed
- [ ] Maps: past/passive tenses, compass and spatial vocabulary, changes grouped by area
- [ ] 3–4 paragraphs including introduction (standard: Intro · Overview · Body 1 · Body 2)
- [ ] 165–185 words in ~20 minutes
