# IELTS Writing Task 1 — Templates by Visual Type (Band 8–9)

> Fill-in templates for every Task 1 visual. Use them as **scaffolding**, not scripts to memorise —
> vary the wording across essays, and never write a template phrase that does not fit the data.
> Bracketed items `[...]` are the parts you replace.

---

## Universal 4-paragraph skeleton

```
P1  The provided [type] illustrates information regarding [topic] [time period/place].
    [Units — e.g. Figures are given in millions of pounds.]

P2  Overall, it is evident that [overall trend / main change], while [second big-picture point].
    [Optional: Another interesting point is that...]

P3  Looking at the details, it can be observed that [group A: start → movement → high/low].
    [Data support + comparison.]

P4  Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that [group B / second period].
    [Data support + ending.]
```

**Rules:** overview has **no exact figures** · 165–185 words · no bullets · no opinion · no contractions.

### "3 paragraphs or 4?" — clearing up the confusion

Task 1 has **three logical parts**, not a fixed number of physical paragraphs:

| Logical part | What it does |
|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | says what the visual shows |
| 2. Overview | the big picture — the most important part |
| 3. Body (details) | supports the overview with data |

The **Body is normally split into two paragraphs**, giving **4 physical paragraphs in total**
(Intro · Overview · Body 1 · Body 2). That is what your teacher wrote in class 1
("4 paragraphs according to the format"), and what IELTS Liz recommends: *"There are four
main paragraphs... Body Paragraph 3 (not usual, but occasionally possible)."*

A **3-physical-paragraph version is also valid**: combine Introduction + Overview into one
paragraph, then keep two body paragraphs. The overview must still start with a clear
`Overall...` statement so the examiner can find it. IELTS marks **logical organisation and a
clearly visible overview**, not a fixed paragraph count — writing everything as one block
would cap Coherence & Cohesion around Band 5.

---


## One fixed linker set — use it in every essay

You write **only one Task 1 response** in the exam, so consistency beats variety. Memorise this
single frame and use it for every visual type. The type-specific words below (e.g. `culminates in`
for processes, `was demolished` for maps) are **content language**, not alternative frames.

| Position | Fixed frame | Contrast word |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | The provided [type] illustrates information regarding [topic]... | `while` (to link two visuals) |
| **Overview** | Overall, it is evident that [big picture], while [second point]. | `whereas` |
| **Body 1** | Looking at the details, it can be observed that [group A]. | `while` |
| **Body 2** | Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that [group B]. | `On the other hand` / `whereas` |
| **End of body** | ...`As a result` / `Consequently` / `Therefore`... | — |

**Why these:** they are your teacher's frames, they are unambiguous, and using the same ones
under time pressure prevents hesitation. Do **not** swap them for `In summary`,
`Broadly speaking`, `Turning to...` etc. — save your attention for the data.

## 1. Line graph

**Introduction**
> The line graph illustrates changes in [what was measured] in [places/categories] between
> [year1] and [year2], with projections for [future years].

**Overview**
> Overall, it is evident that [category A] appears/ is expected to remain the highest throughout, whereas
> [category B] is forecast to overtake [category C] after [year]. All four categories are
> predicted to [direction] by [final year].

**Body 1 — actual data**
> Looking at the details, it can be observed that [A] rose steadily from about [figure] in [year] to
> roughly [figure] in [year]. [B], by contrast, fluctuated, peaking at around [figure] in
> [year] before dipping to [figure]. [C] started lowest at just [figure], but climbed
> consistently to reach [figure] over the same period.

**Body 2 — predictions**
> Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that [A] is likely to level off near [figure], whereas [C] is set to rise
> sharply to [figure], overtaking [B]. [D] is expected to remain the lowest, increasing only
> gradually from [figure] to [figure].

**Useful language:** `peaked at` · `bottomed out at` · `overtake` · `level off` · `is forecast/predicted to` · `whereas` · `by contrast`

**Traps:** don't list every data point · separate actual figures from predictions · don't use past tense for future data.

## 2. Bar chart

**Introduction**
> The bar chart compares [what was measured] across [categories] in [years].

**Overview**
> Overall, it is evident that [category] consistently recorded the highest figures, while [category] remained the
> lowest. A notable feature is that [biggest change / crossover].

**Body 1**
> Looking at the details, it can be observed that in [first year] [category] stood at [figure], compared with [figure] for [category]. By
> [year], it had risen/fallen to [figure], whereas [category] [movement].

**Body 2**
> Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that [category] [movement] from [figure] to [figure] over
> the period. Meanwhile, [category] remained almost unchanged at around [figure].

**Useful language:** `twice as high as` · `roughly three times the figure for` · `marginally higher than` · `in contrast to`

**Traps:** read the legend carefully · don't compare categories from different years as if they were the same series.

---

## 3. Pie chart

**Introduction**
> The pie charts show how [something] was distributed in [year1] and [year2].

**Overview**
> Overall, it is evident that [category] accounted for the largest proportion in both years, while [category]
> represented the smallest share. The most striking change was [change].

**Body 1**
> Looking at the details, it can be observed that in [year1] [category] made up [X]% of the total, followed by [category] at [Y]% and
> [category] at [Z]%.

**Body 2**
> Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that by [year2] the share of [category] had risen/fallen from [X]% to [Y]%, whereas [category]
> [movement] from [X]% to [Y]%. [Category] remained unchanged at [X]%.

**Useful language:** `accounted for` · `made up` · `represented` · `the largest/smallest proportion` · `a marginal increase`

**Traps:** percentages must total 100 · don't say "increased by 10%" when the share fell from 20% to 10%.

## 4. Table

**Introduction**
> The table provides information about [what] in [place] in [year1] and [year2]. Figures are
> given in [units].

**Overview**
> Overall, it is evident that [category] was the main [source/item] in both years, whereas [category] [point].
> The total [increased/decreased] from [figure] to [figure].

**Body 1**
> Looking at the details, it can be observed that [category] rose from [figure] to [figure], while [category]
> increased from [figure] to [figure]. [Category] remained almost unchanged at [figure].

**Body 2**
> Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that the total [rose/fell] from [figure] to [figure]. The biggest contributor was [category],
> providing [figure] in [year1] and [figure] in [year2].

**Traps:** read row and column headings first · keep units consistent · don't confuse a row total with a category figure.

## 5. Mixed charts (table + pie, or two different visuals)

**Introduction**
> The provided [type 1] illustrates information regarding [main feature of type 1], while the
> [type 2] displays data about [main feature of type 2]. [Units for each.]

**Overview**
> Overall, it is evident that [main point of type 1], whereas [summary of type 2].

**Body 1 — type 1 only**
> Looking at the details, it can be observed that [type 1]: [description with figures].

**Body 2 — type 2 only**
> Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that the [type 2] shows [description with percentages/second measure].

**Rule:** one visual per body paragraph. Link the two with `while` (intro) and `whereas` (overview).
**Trap:** never mix £m figures and percentages in the same paragraph.

## 6. Process diagram

**Introduction**
> The diagram illustrates the [number] stages involved in [the manufacture/production of X].

**Overview**
> Overall, it is evident that the process begins with [first stage] and culminates in [final stage], and the whole
> operation is [fully mechanised / carried out manually].

**Body 1**
> Looking at the details, it can be observed that at the first stage [input] is delivered to [place]. It is then fed into [equipment], where
> [action] takes place, before [next stage]. These are subsequently [action] into [product].

**Body 2**
> Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that in the following stage [item] is [action] and then [action]. Finally, [final stage],
> completing the process before [distribution/use].

**Useful language:** present simple passive · `then` · `subsequently` · `in the following stage` · `finally`

**Traps:** `equipment` is uncountable · no opinion · no human actor needed · stages in exact order.

---

## 7. Maps (then vs now)

**Introduction**
> The two maps illustrate how [place] and its surrounding area changed between [year1] and [year2].

**Overview**
> Overall, it is evident that the area underwent [substantial/significant] change: [change 1], [change 2], and
> [change 3].

**Body 1 — the site itself**
> Looking at the details, it can be observed that [feature] was expanded/renamed, while [feature]
> remained unchanged. A new [feature] was constructed [location].

**Body 2 — the surroundings**
> Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that [old feature] was demolished and replaced by [new feature].
> [New feature] was built in the [north-east / south] corner, and [road] remained in the same
> position.

**Useful language:** past simple passive · `was/were constructed / demolished / renamed / relocated / expanded` · `in the north-east corner` · `on the south side` · `in their place`

**Traps:** use past tense, not present · be precise about direction · don't invent changes not shown.

## Template discipline (Band 8–9)

1. **Never memorise whole essays** — memorised templates collapse when the data changes.
2. **Keep the same frames** every time — you write only one essay, so rely on the fixed set above.
3. **One template phrase per paragraph** is enough. Too many fixed phrases = mechanical cohesion.
4. **Fill with precise data** — the template is only the skeleton; the marks are in the accurate selection and comparison of key features.
5. **Check before submitting:** overview present? no figures in it? all series covered? 165–185 words?
