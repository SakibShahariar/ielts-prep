# IELTS Writing Task 1 — Templates by Visual Type (Band 8–9)

> Each type now includes: **the example visual (as data)** → **a full 165–185-word model using the
> fixed frame** → **what to do when the visual has multiple series**. Bracketed items in the
> skeleton are the parts you replace; the models show them filled in.
> Learn the patterns — never memorise a model.

---

## Fixed frame (use this for every type)

```
P1  The provided [type] illustrates information regarding [topic] [period/place].
    [Units.]

P2  Overall, it is evident that [big picture], while [second big-picture point].

P3  Looking at the details, it can be observed that [group A]. [Data + comparison.]

P4  Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that [group B]. [Data + ending.]
```

**Fixed linker set:** `while` (intro) · `whereas` / `while` (overview/body contrast) ·
`On the other hand` (second visual) · `As a result / Consequently / Therefore` (results).
Referencing (`this trend`, `the former`, `such a rise`) does more work than extra linkers.

> "3 paragraphs or 4?" — Task 1 has **3 logical parts** (Intro, Overview, Body). The Body is
> normally split into two paragraphs → **4 physical paragraphs**, which your teacher prescribed in
> class 1. Combining Intro + Overview into one paragraph is a valid 3-paragraph variant; the
> overview must still be clearly marked with `Overall`.

# 1. Line graph — single line

## Example visual: Sales (units) for Tesco, 2019

| Month | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | 95,000 | 110,000 | 125,000 | 130,000 | 140,000 | 190,000 | 220,000 | 250,000 | 270,000 | 285,000 | 295,000 | 305,000 |

Shape: `▁▂▂▃▃▅▆▇▇███` — gentle rise to May, steep climb to August, then gradual growth.

## Filled model

The provided line graph illustrates information regarding sales of Tesco in 2019. Figures are
given in units.

Overall, it is evident that sales rose consistently throughout the year, while the steepest growth
occurred between May and August. By December, the figure was more than three times its January level.

Looking at the details, it can be observed that sales started at approximately 95,000 units in
January and climbed gradually to about 140,000 by May. This steady early rise was followed by a
much sharper increase: by July the figure had reached 220,000, a gain of roughly 80,000 units in
just two months.

Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that the upward trend continued at a slower pace in the
final four months. Sales rose from around 250,000 in August to just under 305,000 in December, with
the smallest monthly increase occurring between November and December.

*(≈175 words)*

**Language:** `climbed gradually` · `sharper increase` · `upward trend continued` · `just under`.

# 2. Line graph — MULTIPLE lines (the common exam case)

## Example visual: Urban population in four Asian countries, 1970–2040

| Year | Philippines | Malaysia | Thailand | Indonesia |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1970 | 32% | 30% | 19% | 14% |
| 1980 | 34% | 40% | 23% | 16% |
| 1990 | 48% | 45% | 30% | 25% |
| 2000 | 45% | 60% | 30% | 30% |
| 2010 | 42% | 70% | 32% | 42% |
| 2020 | 45% | 75% | 33% | 52% |
| 2030* | 50% | 81% | 40% | 60% |
| 2040* | 56% | 83% | 49% | 64% |

\*projected. Four lines; one crosses another; one fluctuates; one grows slowly.

## How to handle 3–4 lines

**Do NOT describe line by line.** Group by **pattern**:

| Group | Line | Pattern to name |
|---|---|---|
| Leader | Malaysia | highest from 1980, steady climb, levels off |
| Overtaker | Indonesia | lowest start, fastest growth, crosses Philippines after 2020 |
| Fluctuator | Philippines | peak in 1990, dip to 2010, recovery |
| Laggard | Thailand | slowest growth, stays lowest |

**Overview must contain:** the leader, the overtaker/crossover, the overall direction.
**Body 1:** the leader + the fluctuator. **Body 2:** the overtaker + the laggard + projections.

**Crossover language:** `overtake` · `converge` · `narrow the gap` · `the former` · `the latter` ·
`whereas` · `by contrast`.

## Filled model

The provided line graph illustrates information regarding the proportion of the population living
in cities in four Asian countries between 1970 and 2020, with projections to 2040. Figures are
given as percentages.

Overall, it is evident that Malaysia remained the most urbanised country for most of the period,
while Indonesia is forecast to overtake the Philippines after 2020. All four countries are expected
to finish higher than they began.

Looking at the details, it can be observed that Malaysia rose steadily from about 30% in 1970 to
roughly 75% in 2020, with no significant reversals. The Philippines followed a less consistent
path, peaking at around 48% in 1990 before dipping to 42% in 2010 and recovering slightly to 45% a
decade later.

Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that Indonesia began as the least urbanised nation at just
14%, but climbed consistently to 52% by 2020. Thailand, by contrast, changed only gradually, moving
from 19% to 33% over the same fifty years. The projections show Malaysia levelling off near 83% by
2040, while Indonesia is set to reach around 64%.

*(≈190 words)*

## Second multi-line example — three lines with a crossover

| Year | Attraction A | Attraction B | Attraction C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 40,000 | 25,000 | 25,000 |
| 2015 | 35,000 | 30,000 | 40,000 |
| 2020 | 30,000 | 45,000 | 38,000 |

**Overview sentence:** *Overall, it is evident that visitor numbers at A declined throughout,
while B overtook both A and C to become the most visited attraction by 2020.*
**Body 1:** A (declining) vs B (rising) — mention the crossover around 2017.
**Body 2:** C (peak in 2015, slight fall) + final ranking.

**If one line is flat while others move:** name the stability (`remained almost unchanged at around
X`) in the overview, then use `whereas` to contrast it with the moving lines.

# 3. Bar chart — single and grouped

## Example visual: Student involvement in selected sports

| Class | Soccer | Cricket | Basketball |
|---|---|---|---|
| VIII | 25% | 55% | 20% |
| IX | 30% | 45% | 25% |
| X | 32% | 40% | 28% |

## Filled model

The provided bar chart illustrates information regarding student involvement in three sports
across three school classes. Figures are given as percentages.

Overall, it is evident that cricket was the most popular sport in every class, while soccer and
basketball both became gradually more popular. By Class X, the gap between the three sports had
narrowed considerably.

Looking at the details, it can be observed that cricket attracted 55% of Class VIII students, but
this figure fell steadily to 45% in Class IX and 40% in Class X. Soccer, on the other hand, rose
from 25% to 30% and then to 32% over the same three classes.

Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that basketball remained the least popular sport throughout,
although it also increased, from 20% in Class VIII to 28% in Class X. As a result, the difference
between the most and least popular sports fell from 35 percentage points to just 12.

*(≈175 words)*

**Multi-series rule:** compare **within each category** (class), not bar by bar across the chart;
name the overall convergence/divergence in the overview.

# 4. Pie chart — single and multiple

## Example visual: US spending patterns, 2018

| Cars | Recreation | Food | Restaurants | Petrol | Furniture | Books |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 48% | 13% | 12% | 8% | 8% | 7% | 4% |

## Filled model

The provided pie chart illustrates information regarding spending patterns in the United States in
2018. Figures are given as percentages of total expenditure.

Overall, it is evident that cars accounted for nearly half of all spending, while books represented
the smallest proportion. The remaining categories were relatively evenly distributed.

Looking at the details, it can be observed that cars made up 48% of expenditure, a figure more than
three times that of the next largest category. Recreation and food accounted for 13% and 12%
respectively, together making up a quarter of the total.

Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that restaurants, petrol and furniture each took a similar
share, at 8%, 8% and 7%. Books, by contrast, represented just 4% of spending — a marginal figure
compared with the amount allocated to cars.

*(≈165 words)*

## Two pie charts (change over time)

Example: EU exports to Australia 2015 vs 2020 — Belgium 25%→20% · UK 25%→17% · France 8%→9% ·
Germany 15%→25% · Italy 10%→5% · Spain 6%→11% · Sweden 2%→5% · Others 5%→8%.

**Overview:** *Overall, it is evident that Germany overtook Belgium and the UK as the largest
exporter, while several smaller categories increased their shares.*
**Body 1:** 2015 distribution (Belgium and UK level at 25%; Germany 15%).
**Body 2:** 2020 changes (Germany rose to 25%; Belgium and UK fell; Spain and Sweden doubled).
Use `whereas`, `while`, `narrowed`, `overtook`, `remained unchanged`.

# 5. Table

## Example visual: Distribution of students over three universities

| Department | York | U of T | Monash |
|---|---|---|---|
| BBA | 800 | 600 | 550 |
| Economics | 350 | 200 | 200 |
| English | 280 | 150 | 150 |
| Architecture | 120 | 200 | 150 |
| Pharmacy | 65 | 220 | 200 |

## Filled model

The provided table illustrates information regarding the distribution of students across three
departments at three universities. Figures are given as numbers of students.

Overall, it is evident that BBA was the largest department at two of the universities, while
Pharmacy was notably larger at U of T and Monash than at York. York had the most marked variation
between departments.

Looking at the details, it can be observed that York recorded 800 BBA students, more than twice
the number at Monash and substantially higher than the 600 at U of T. Economics and English were
also largest at York, at 350 and 280 students respectively.

Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that the pattern was reversed in Pharmacy, where York had
just 65 students compared with 220 at U of T and 200 at Monash. Architecture was fairly evenly
distributed, with figures ranging from 120 to 200 across the three institutions.

*(≈180 words)*

**Large tables:** never list every cell. Group by **largest**, **smallest**, and **notable
exceptions**; use `respectively` for matched lists.

# 6. Mixed charts (two different visuals)

## Example visual: Police budget, 2017 and 2018

**Table — sources of funding (£m)**

| Source | 2017 | 2018 |
|---|---|---|
| National Government | 175.5 | ~177.4 |
| Local Taxes | 91.2 | 102.7 |
| Other sources | 38.0 | 38.5 |
| **Total** | **304.7** | **318.6** |

**Pie charts — spending:** salaries of officers/staff = largest share in both years (about
three-quarters in 2017, around three-fifths in 2018); remainder split between technology and
building and transport.

## Filled model

The provided table illustrates information regarding the sources of funding for a police
department in Britain, while the two pie charts display data about how the budget was spent in 2017
and 2018. Funding figures are given in millions of pounds and expenditure as percentages.

Overall, it is evident that the national government was the chief source of income in both years,
whereas the majority of the budget was consistently allocated to salaries of officers and staff.

Looking at the details, it can be observed that the total budget rose from £304.7 million in 2017
to £318.6 million in 2018. The national government contributed approximately £175.5 million in the
initial year, while local taxes increased from £91.2 million to £102.7 million.

Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that salaries accounted for the largest share of spending in
both years, although the proportion fell from roughly three-quarters to about three-fifths. The
remainder was divided between technology and building and transport.

*(≈180 words)*

**Rule:** one visual per body paragraph; `while` in the intro, `whereas` in the overview,
`On the other hand` for body 2. Never mix £m figures with percentages in one paragraph.

# 7. Process diagram

## Example visual: Brick making

```
digger (clay) → roller + metal grid (sand + water added) → mould + wire cutter (bricks)
→ drying oven (24–48 hrs) → kiln 1 (moderate 200–980°C) → kiln 2 (high 870–1300°C)
→ cooling chamber (48–72 hrs) → packaging → delivery
```

## Filled model

The provided diagram illustrates information regarding the process of manufacturing bricks.

Overall, it is evident that the process consists of nine stages, commencing with the extraction of
clay and culminating in the delivery of the finished bricks.

Looking at the details, it can be observed that clay is first dug by a mechanical digger and then
passed through a roller, where sand and water are added. The mixture is subsequently shaped in a
mould and cut into individual bricks by a wire cutter.

Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that the bricks are then dried in an oven for 24 to 48 hours
before being fired in two kilns: first at a moderate temperature of 200–980°C and then at a high
temperature of 870–1300°C. After cooling for a further 48 to 72 hours, they are packaged and sent
for delivery.

*(≈170 words)*

**Branching processes** (e.g. recruitment with pass/fail paths): group by successful route vs
early exits; use `whereas` and `if... then...`.
**Cycles:** end with `the cycle then repeats itself`.

# 8. Maps

## Example visual: Tilden Airport, 1985 vs 2015

| Feature | 1985 | 2015 |
|---|---|---|
| Airport name | Tilden Airport | Tilden International Airport |
| Runways | one | two (new diagonal runway) |
| International terminal | small | expanded |
| Domestic terminal / car park | unchanged | broadly unchanged |
| Transport | none | bus and light rail station added |
| Support services | original site | relocated east, enlarged |
| Surroundings | Johnson's Farm, factories | business park, housing estate, hotel & conference centre |

## Filled model

The provided maps illustrate information regarding the development of Tilden Airport and its
surrounding area over a period of 30 years, between 1985 and 2015.

Overall, it is evident that the site underwent substantial redevelopment, while several original
features, including the domestic terminal and car park, remained broadly unchanged.

Looking at the details, it can be observed that the airport was renamed Tilden International Airport
and a second, diagonal runway was constructed. The international terminal was expanded
considerably, and a bus and light rail station was built beside the car park.

Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that Johnson's Farm was replaced by a business park and a
housing estate, while the factories on the southern side were demolished. In their place, a hotel
and conference centre was built, and Airport Support Services were relocated eastwards and enlarged.

*(≈170 words)*

**Tense rule:** `in 1985` → past simple passive; `by 2015` → past perfect passive.

## Final checklist

- [ ] Overview present, figure-free, clearly marked `Overall`
- [ ] Key features selected and grouped — multi-line charts grouped by **pattern**, not line-by-line
- [ ] Every series/visual covered; crossovers and extremes named
- [ ] Data supports each comparison
- [ ] Fixed frames used consistently; no memorised essays
- [ ] 165–185 words; no bullets; no opinion
