> **Task B** — Istanbul Promo Plus sales line graph, 2007.
>
> Transcribed and upgraded. Some figures on the board are hard to read in the photo, so the

---

## 1. What class 2 added

- **Intro:** name the chart type, the topic, the period — and state the **unit** if given
  (e.g. "The sales were calculated in millions of Turkish Lira").
- **Overview:** `Overall, it is evident that...` plus a second big-picture point with
  `Another interesting point is that...`
- The overview can describe: a **fluctuating trend** in one series while others rise; which
  series was **most popular at the end**; start vs end comparison. **No exact figures** in the overview.
- **Body 1:** `Looking at the details, it can be observed that...`
- **Body 2:** `Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that...`
- **Contrast:** `while`, `On the other hand`.
- **Sequencing:** `This was followed by...`, `Furthermore`, `Finally`, `after which`,
  `in the last two years`.
- **New vocabulary:** fluctuate · relatively unchanged · fairly equal · stood at · rose slightly ·
  a significant increase · plummeted dramatically · a little over · nearly doubled ·
  a small climb · levelled off · remained almost static · hit the peak point · close to ·
  just above · at their highest · the weakest figure.

## 2. Task A — New York airports bar chart (1995–2000)

**Prompt as written on the board:**
> *The chart below shows the number of travellers using three major airports in New York City between 1995 and 2000.*

Three series: **John F. Kennedy**, **La Guardia**, **Newark** (millions of passengers per year).

### Teacher's model — upgraded to Band 8–9

**Introduction**
> The provided bar chart illustrates information regarding how many people travelled through
> three major airports in New York City over a period of six years, from 1995 to 2000.

**Overview**
> Overall, it is evident that over the time span there was a fluctuating trend in the number
> of passengers using John F. Kennedy airport, while the other two airports saw an upward
> trend. Another interesting point is that La Guardia had become the busiest airport by the end.

**Body 1**
> Looking at the details, it can be observed that the number of travellers at John F. Kennedy
> airport started at approximately 26 million in 1995, before rising sharply to its peak of
> roughly 47 million. The figure then dipped to nearly 22 million. On the other hand, La
> Guardia began at about 35 million in the initial year and then rose steadily for the next
> three years, reaching just above 45 million. By 2000, it had peaked at close to 68 million
> passengers.

**Body 2**
> Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that Newark started at the lowest point of about 16
> million passengers in the first year. Furthermore, the figure escalated significantly to
> around 40 million by 1998. Finally, over the last two years it remained stable at
> approximately 41 million travellers.

### Corrections made to the board version

| Board wording | Issue | Band 8–9 version |
|---|---|---|
| `there was a fluctuate trend` | Wrong word form | `there was a fluctuating trend` |
| `through three major airport` | Plural noun | `through three major airports` |
| `passengers who travel via` | Unnecessary relative clause | `passengers using / travelling via` |
| `increased remarkably to reach the highest point of about 47 million than US 15 million` | Garbled | `rising sharply to its peak of roughly 47 million` |
| `Figure dipped to nearly 22 million` | Missing article | `The figure dipped to nearly 22 million` |
| `hit the peak point` | Clumsy | `peaked at` / `reached its peak at` |
| `the trends escalated significantly` | Wrong subject | `the figure escalated significantly` |
| `rose remarkably` used twice | Repetition | `rose sharply` + `rose steadily` |
| `La Guardia was the most popular at the end` | Vague | `La Guardia had become the busiest airport by the end` |

## 3. Task B — Istanbul Promo Plus sales line graph (2007)

**Prompt as written on the board:**
> *The chart below gives information about Istanbul Promo Plus sales in 2007.*
> Unit: millions of Turkish Lira. Months: January–December.

### Teacher's model — upgraded to Band 8–9

**Introduction**
> The provided line graph illustrates information regarding the sales of Istanbul Promo Plus
> in a particular year, 2007. The sales were calculated in millions of Turkish Lira.

**Overview**
> Overall, it is evident that sales fluctuated considerably over the period. They remained
> relatively unchanged across the year, as January and December figures were fairly equal.
> In addition, sales were at their highest in March, while the lowest figures were recorded
> in July and September.

**Body 1**
> Looking at the details, it can be observed that in the first month of 2007, Promo Plus sales
> stood at 200 million Turkish Lira, before rising slightly to around 225 million in February.
> This was followed by a significant increase in March, when sales climbed to almost 325
> million Turkish Lira.

**Body 2**
> Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that this upward trend was suddenly broken and sales
> plummeted dramatically over the next four months, reaching a little over 100 million Turkish
> Lira in July. Moreover, August saw a considerable rebound to January levels as the figures
> nearly doubled. However, this was not to last: sales dropped again in September back to the
> June and July level. Finally, October brought a modest climb of approximately 80 million
> Turkish Lira, after which sales levelled off and remained almost static over the last two
> months of 2007.

### Corrections made to the board version

| Board wording | Issue | Band 8–9 version |
|---|---|---|
| `sales were at their highest in March, while the weakest sales figure could be witnessed in July and September` | Awkward passive | `the lowest figures were recorded in July and September` |
| `It remained relatively unchanged in 2007 as January and December sales were fairly equal` | Run-on | `They remained relatively unchanged across the year, as January and December figures were fairly equal` |
| `after that February` | Garbled fragment | delete |
| `over the next 4 months` | Style | `over the next four months` |
| `August sales displayed a considerable rise back to January levels` | Repetition of "sales" | `August saw a considerable rebound to January levels` |
| `this was not to last as they dropped again in September to the June level as they were in July` | Garbled | `this was not to last: sales dropped again in September back to the June and July level` |
| `October came with a small climb` | Awkward | `October brought a modest climb` |
| `levelled off and remained almost static over the last two months` | Correct — keep it | `levelled off` + `remained almost static` |

## 4. Reusable sentence bank from class 2

| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|

| **Body — starting** | Looking at the details, it can be observed that... · Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that... · started at approximately... · stood at... · began at the lowest point of about... |

| **Body — comparison** | On the other hand... · while... · as the figures nearly doubled · back to January levels · just above / close to / roughly / approximately |
| **Body — ending** | Finally... · over the last two years it remained stable at... · sales levelled off and remained almost static · after which... |

## 5. Grammar watch-list from class 2

- ❌ `a fluctuate trend` → ✅ `a fluctuating trend` / `fluctuations`
- ❌ `three major airport` → ✅ `three major airports`
- ❌ `Figure dipped` → ✅ `The figure dipped`
- ❌ `than US 15 million` → delete the garbled clause
- ❌ `after that February` → delete the fragment
- ❌ `the trends escalated` → ✅ `the figure escalated`
- Avoid repeating `remarkably` — vary with `sharply / steadily / substantially / dramatically`.

## 6. Band 8–9 checklist for these two tasks

- [ ] Overview gives the big picture with **no exact figures**
- [ ] All three airports / the whole period covered
- [ ] Data supported with `approximately · about · roughly · close to · just above · a little over`
- [ ] Trend vocabulary varied — no repeated `remarkably` or `significant`
- [ ] Plurals, articles and subject–verb agreement correct
- [ ] Cohesion unobtrusive: `while · On the other hand · Furthermore · Finally · after which`
- [ ] Word count 165–185
