# IELTS Writing: Standalone Synonym Bank for Band 8–9

> Companion to `ielts-writing-task-1-notes.md`.
> This file lists **synonyms of each word separately** — not "very + adjective" swaps.

## Golden rule

**Synonyms are rarely interchangeable.** Each word has several *senses* (meanings),
and a synonym that fits one sense may be wrong in another. Band 8 Lexical Resource
requires precision and natural collocation — a wrong-but-impressive word is an
*inappropriacy* and keeps you at Band 7. Always check: **meaning → register → collocation**.

---

## 1. "Very" — standalone synonyms

`very` is an intensifying adverb. Its synonyms fall into two groups:

| Type | Synonyms | Use / warning |
|---|---|---|
| **Degree intensifiers** | extremely · exceptionally · highly · remarkably · exceedingly · intensely · profoundly · tremendously · particularly · especially · unusually | Modify adjectives/adverbs: *extremely important*, *highly likely*, *remarkably similar*. They do **not** modify verbs. |
| **Absolute intensifiers** | absolutely · completely · totally · utterly · entirely · wholly · fully | Use with non-gradable adjectives: *utterly disastrous*, *completely different*. ❌ *very unique*, ❌ *absolutely interesting* (usually). |
| **Informal / avoid in IELTS** | awfully · terribly · dreadfully · frightfully · jolly · pretty | Too conversational or old-fashioned for academic writing. |

Register note: `extremely` and `exceptionally` are safe all-rounders. `highly` collocates
with adjectives such as *likely, effective, competent, relevant, significant*.

## 2. Master synonym table (headword by headword)

| Headword | Synonyms (grouped by sense) | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **good** | high quality: excellent · outstanding · superb · impressive · first-rate<br>beneficial: beneficial · advantageous · favourable · constructive · helpful<br>adequate: satisfactory · acceptable · adequate · decent<br>moral: ethical · virtuous · honourable | `good` is vague; pick the sense you actually mean. |
| **bad** | low quality: poor · inferior · substandard · shoddy<br>harmful: harmful · detrimental · damaging · adverse · injurious<br>serious: serious · severe · grave · acute<br>moral: unethical · wrongful | ❌ *bad influence* → *a detrimental influence*. |
| **important** | crucial · vital · essential · significant · key · pivotal · critical · fundamental · paramount · major | `crucial/vital/essential` are strongest; `significant` also means "noticeable". |
| **big** | large · huge · enormous · vast · massive · substantial · considerable · extensive · major · significant | `large` = neutral/formal; `big` = informal. For data use *substantial*, not *big*. |
| **small** | little · tiny · minor · slight · marginal · modest · limited · minute · compact | `little` often carries feeling; `minor/marginal` for data. |
| **very** | see §1 | Not interchangeable with absolutely/totally in all cases. |
| **show** | illustrate · demonstrate · reveal · indicate · depict · present · display · exhibit · suggest · reveal | `show` + that-clause works; `depict` is for images/charts. |
| **say** | state · claim · argue · assert · maintain · contend · declare · note · point out · report | `claim` implies doubt; `argue` introduces a reasoned position. |
| **think** | believe · consider · argue · maintain · hold the view that · suppose · estimate · presume | Avoid *I think* everywhere; use *I would argue that*. |
| **get** | obtain · acquire · gain · receive · secure · attain · achieve | *get a degree* → *obtain/earn a degree*. |
| **people** | individuals · citizens · the public · society · the population · the community · households · the younger generation | `people` is fine, but vary with a precise group noun. |
| **problem** | issue · challenge · difficulty · obstacle · concern · predicament · drawback · trouble | `issue` is the safest formal substitute. |
| **help** | assist · aid · facilitate · enable · support · promote · contribute to · foster | `facilitate` + noun, not *facilitate someone to do*. |
| **use** | employ · utilise · apply · adopt · make use of · take advantage of · harness | Don't force *utilise* in every sentence; plain `use` is often best. |
| **change** | alter · transform · shift · modify · vary · adjust · convert · undergo a change | `change` is neutral; `transform` implies a large change. |
| **because** | as · since · owing to · due to · given that · in that · for the reason that | `due to/owing to` + noun; `because/as/since` + clause. |
| **so** | therefore · thus · consequently · hence · as a result · accordingly | Mind punctuation: *Therefore, ...* / *...; therefore, ...* |
| **but** | however · nevertheless · nonetheless · whereas · although · though · yet · still | `however` needs a full stop or semicolon before it. |
| **also** | moreover · furthermore · in addition · additionally · equally · likewise · as well | Use sparingly — Band 8 cohesion should be invisible. |
| **more** | further · additional · extra · greater · increased · extra `more and more` → increasingly · a growing number of | `more better` ❌ → `better`. |
| **many** | numerous · a great many · a large number of · multiple · countless · a wide range of | Countable nouns only. |
| **much** | a great deal of · considerable · substantial · abundant · ample | Uncountable nouns only. |
| **a lot of** | numerous · plentiful · abundant · a substantial amount of · a wide range of | Too informal for IELTS writing. |
| **often** | frequently · regularly · commonly · typically · repeatedly · time and again | `often` itself is acceptable; vary with `frequently`. |
| **always** | invariably · consistently · constantly · perpetually · without exception | Strong claims → hedge with *tends to / often*. |
| **never** | rarely · seldom · hardly ever · scarcely · at no time | `rarely/seldom` at the start trigger inversion: *Rarely do we see...* |
| **maybe** | perhaps · possibly · potentially · conceivably | `maybe` = informal; use `perhaps`. |
| **probably** | likely · presumably · in all likelihood · most likely · arguably | *It is likely that...* / *X is likely to...* |
| **nowadays** | currently · in recent years · today · at present · in contemporary society · increasingly | Don't open every essay with *Nowadays*. |
| **thing** | matter · issue · factor · aspect · item · consideration · phenomenon | Almost always replaceable by a precise noun. |
| **way** | method · approach · means · technique · strategy · manner · fashion | *a way to solve* → *an approach to solving*. |
| **make** | create · produce · generate · bring about · cause · lead to · formulate | *make a decision* → *reach a decision*. |
| **give** | provide · offer · supply · deliver · grant · present · contribute | *give importance to* → *attach importance to*. |
| **take** | require · consume · occupy · adopt · capture · undergo | *take measures* → *implement measures*. |
| **work** | function · operate · perform · labour · job · task · employment | Two senses: verb (function) and noun (job/effort). |
| **effect** | impact · influence · consequence · result · outcome · implication · effect | `affect` (verb) ≠ `effect` (noun). Classic error. |
| **reason** | cause · factor · explanation · rationale · motive · justification | *the reason of* ❌ → *the reason for*. |
| **idea** | concept · notion · view · thought · proposal · suggestion · insight | `idea` is vague in essays; prefer `argument` / `proposal`. |
| **opinion** | view · belief · perspective · stance · position · judgement · standpoint | *in my opinion* → *I would argue that*. |
| **increase** | rise · grow · climb · surge · soar · escalate · expand · boost | See Task 1 notes for data verbs. |
| **decrease** | decline · fall · drop · reduce · diminish · plummet · shrink | `decrease` can be verb or noun. |

## 5. Common traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "very unique", "more better" | Double marking / wrong comparative | `unique`, `better` |
| `Nowadays, in my opinion I think...` | Redundant | `I would argue that...` |
| `people is / the number of people are` | Agreement | `people are`, `the number of people is` |
| `affect` vs `effect` | Word class confusion | `affect` = verb, `effect` = noun |
| `a lot of` in formal writing | Too informal | `numerous` / `a substantial number of` |
| `utilise` in every sentence | Inflated, unnatural | Use plain `use` where it fits |
| `in conclusion, to sum up` together | Redundant | Pick one |
| Over-strong claims (`always`, `never`, `completely`) | Over-generalisation → TR cap at 7 | Hedge: `often`, `tends to`, `in many cases` |

## 6. Quick practice

Replace the **headword itself** with an accurate standalone synonym:

1. *This is a **good** approach.* → **This is a sound / effective approach.**
2. *It has a **bad** effect on health.* → **It has a detrimental / adverse effect on health.**
3. *Education is **important**.* → **Education is crucial / vital.**
4. *The chart **shows** the trend.* → **The chart illustrates / depicts the trend.**
5. *Many **people** believe this.* → **Many citizens / individuals believe this.**
6. *It is **very** common.* → **It is extremely / particularly common.**
7. *This **problem** needs attention.* → **This issue / challenge needs attention.**

**Habit:** after every essay, circle `very`, `good`, `bad`, `important`, `thing`, `people`, `show`, `problem` and replace at least half with a precise standalone synonym — without changing your meaning.
