## 0. What Band 8–9 actually requires (read this first)

| Criterion | Band 7 | **Band 8** | **Band 9** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Task Achievement** | Covers requirements; clear overview; key features highlighted but could be more fully illustrated | Covers all requirements **appropriately, relevantly and sufficiently**; key features **skilfully selected**, clearly presented, highlighted **and illustrated**; occasional lapses only | All requirements fully satisfied; extremely rare lapses |
| **Coherence & Cohesion** | Logically organised, clear progression, a few lapses | Message followed **with ease**; cohesion well managed; paragraphing sufficient and appropriate | Message followed **effortlessly**; cohesion rarely attracts attention; paragraphing skilful |
| **Lexical Resource** | Some flexibility/precision; awareness of style and collocation; inappropriacies occur | **Skilful** use of uncommon/idiomatic items; accurate and appropriate; wide range; only occasional inaccuracies | Full flexibility, precise and very natural control |
| **Grammar Range & Accuracy** | Variety of complex structures; a few errors that do not impede | Majority of sentences **error-free**; errors **non-systematic**; punctuation well managed | Full flexibility and control; errors extremely rare |

**The Band 7 → 8 trap:** repeated *systematic* errors (articles, prepositions, subject–verb agreement) keep you at 7 even if your ideas are good. At Band 8 the majority of your sentences must be error-free, and the errors you do make must be non-systematic.

## 1. Exam mechanics (from your teacher, upgraded)

- **Time:** 20 minutes. **Minimum:** 150 words.
- **Band 8–9 target:** **165–185 words.** There is no penalty for writing more, but you need enough data to illustrate key features. Padding lowers LR quality.
- **Format:** **4 paragraphs** — Introduction · Overview · Body 1 · Body 2. Paragraphing only becomes a scoring factor at Bands 8–9, so make paragraph boundaries clean and purposeful.
- **Full connected text only.** No bullets, notes, sub-headings, greeting or sign-off.
- **Register:** formal / impersonal. **No contractions** (`does not`, not `doesn't`).
- **Person:** no **first person** (*I, we, my, our*) and no **second person** (*you, your*). Your teacher wrote "no 1st, 2nd, 3rd person" — that is too broad: **impersonal third person is correct and necessary** (*it, the figure, the chart, they* for groups of people).
- **No needless repetition** of adjectives, adverbs and nouns — but never force an inaccurate synonym just to avoid a repeat. Precision beats variety at Band 8.
- **Never copy the rubric.** Paraphrase the question; only unavoidable key terms (e.g. proper nouns, units) may stay.

### Task types in the syllabus
1. Line graph
2. Bar graph / column graph
3. Pie chart
4. Data table
5. Mixed / combined charts
6. Process diagram
7. Maps

## 2. The 4-paragraph architecture

| Paragraph | Job | Word budget | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Introduction** | Paraphrase the prompt + state the time period / categories | 25–30 | ~2 min |
| **2. Overview** | The big picture: overall trend + extremes + start–end. **No figures.** | 25–35 | ~2 min |
| **3. Body 1** | First major trend/group: data state → comparison → difference | 55–65 | ~6 min |
| **4. Body 2** | Second trend/group: partial trends → exceptions → approximation | 55–65 | ~6 min |
| Check | Articles, agreement, punctuation, spelling | — | ~3 min |

Plan before writing: **3 minutes** to identify the 2–3 key features and the overview. A wrong overview caps you at Band 5, so this is the highest-value three minutes in the exam.

## 3. Introduction: paraphrasing the prompt

Your teacher's frame: *"The provided [type] shows information regarding [topic]"* — useful as a starting point, but upgrade it.

**Better reporting verbs:** `illustrates` · `depicts` · `presents` · `compares` · `outlines` · `charts` · `records` · `reveals` · `provides a breakdown of`

**Replace vague phrasing:**
- `shows information regarding` → `shows the figures for` / `details changes in` / `gives data on`
- `the question` → delete it; paraphrase the actual topic instead.

**Time expressions:**
- `between 2005 and 2010` → `over the six-year period from 2005 to 2010` / `across the period 2005–2010`
- `from 2005 to 2010` = within that same period. When you say "a six-year period", you are counting the data points (2005, 2006, 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010).

**Countries and groups:**
- `UK, USA, UAE` → `three nations, namely the UK, the USA and the UAE` / `in multiple countries, namely...`

**Example (using your line graph):**
> *The line graph illustrates changes in the price of a commodity between 2005 and 2010.*

## 4. The Overview — "a summary within a summary"

This is the single most important paragraph. Band 7 requires a **clear** overview; Band 8 requires it to be accurate, selective and smoothly integrated.

### What to include (pick 2–3)
- **Overall direction:** upward / downward / broadly flat / volatile / mixed
- **Extremes:** `peak` · `maximum` · `the highest figure` · `the strongest performance` · `the most popular` ↔ `the lowest` · `minimum` · `the weakest` · `the least popular`
- **Start–end comparison:** how the first and last years differ
- **Overall pattern / tendency:** convergence, divergence, a crossover, a plateau, a striking anomaly

### What to exclude
- Exact figures (save them for the body)
- Every data point
- Your opinion or explanation of *why*

### Frames (use ONE, and vary across essays)
- *Overall, it is evident that...*
- *In summary, ...*
- *Looking at the broad picture, ...*
- *Broadly speaking, ...*
- *The most striking feature is that...*

**Example (your line graph):**
> *Overall, the price followed a consistently upward path, more than quintupling over the six-year period. The steepest growth occurred between 2006 and 2008, while the final two years saw a much more gradual rise.*

⚠️ Do not open every essay with "Overall, it is evident that" — at Band 8, cohesion should feel natural, not formulaic.

## 5. Body paragraphs: the 6 moves

Your teacher's sequence → upgraded with examples.

### Move 1 — Data state (state the starting value)
- *The price of potatoes **stood at** $10 in 2005, the initial year.*
- *At the outset, the figure was $10.*
- *X **accounted for** 25% of the total.*

### Move 2 — Data comparison (adjective + noun / verb + adverb)
- `a significant increase` / `increased significantly`
- `twice as high as` · `roughly three times the figure for` · `marginally higher than` · `in contrast to`
- *The figure for X was **twice as high as** that for Y.*

### Move 3 — Data difference (**by vs to** — critical)
- `increased significantly **by** $40` → the **amount of change**
- `increased significantly **to** $50` → the **final value**
- *The price rose sharply **by** $10 between 2006 and 2007, reaching $25.*

### Move 4 — Partial trends
- *After a modest rise in 2006, the figure surged...*
- *Having peaked at $41 in 2008, the price then...*
- *The upward trend was briefly interrupted in 2009.*

### Move 5 — Approximation
`approximately` · `roughly` · `around` · `about` · `nearly` · `just under` · `just over` · `approaching` · `in the region of` · `close to`
Use approximation when a value must be read off a graph; use exact figures when they are printed in a table.

### Move 6 — Linker / connection
Keep linkers **invisible** at Band 8: a few precise ones (`by contrast`, `whereas`, `subsequently`, `thereafter`, `by comparison`) are better than a mechanical chain of `Firstly / Secondly / In conclusion`. Anaphoric referencing (`this trend`, `the former`, `the latter`, `such a rise`) is a stronger cohesion signal.

**Natural body openers (from your teacher, refined):**
- *Looking at the details, it can be observed that...*
- *Upon closer inspection, it is apparent that...*
- *Turning to the individual years, ...*
- *In terms of the two leading categories, ...*

## 6. Language toolkit (corrected and completed)

### 6.1 Direction: upward

| Verbs | Nouns / phrases |
|---|---|
| rose to · climbed to · increased to · grew to · went up to | a rise · an increase · a growth · an upturn |
| surged to · soared to · rocketed to · escalated to · shot up to | a surge · a leap · a sharp upward trend |
| peaked at · reached a peak of | a peak · a high point |
| recovered to · rebounded to | a recovery · a rebound |

⚠️ Your teacher listed **"managed to"** — remove it. It means "succeeded in doing something with effort" and is not used for data movement.
⚠️ **"boomed to"** is unnatural: write `boomed` (no *to*) or `a boom in sales`. Prefer `soared to` / `rocketed to` when you need a *to*.

### 6.2 Direction: downward

| Verbs | Nouns / phrases |
|---|---|
| decreased to · declined to · fell to · dropped to · dipped to · went down to | a decrease · a decline · a fall · a drop |
| slumped to · plummeted to · tumbled to · nosedived to | a slump · a steep decline |
| bottomed out at | a low point · a trough |

### 6.3 Direction: stable / fluctuating

| Meaning | Verbs | Nouns |
|---|---|---|
| Stable | levelled out · levelled off · flattened out · remained stable/steady/unchanged/static at · stayed constant at · maintained the same level at · did not change | a levelling-off · a plateau · stability · no change |
| Fluctuating | fluctuated · oscillated · rose and fell | a fluctuation · fluctuations · **a fluctuating trend** |

⚠️ Your teacher wrote **"a fluctuant trend"** — not idiomatic. Use **"a fluctuating trend"**.

### 6.4 Degree: adjective + adverb

| Degree | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | significant · dramatic · remarkable · drastic · marked · substantial · noticeable | significantly · dramatically · remarkably · drastically · markedly · substantially · noticeably |
| **Medium** | moderate · steady · considerable | moderately · steadily · considerably |
| **Low** | slight · small · minor · marginal · tiny | slightly · marginally |

**Collocation rules (Band 8 LR):**
- `verb + adverb`: *rose sharply*, *increased significantly*
- `adjective + noun`: *a sharp rise*, *a significant increase*
- ❌ *a sharp rose* / *increased significant*

**Spellings to watch:** `dramatically`, `remarkably`, `significantly`, `noticeably`, `fluctuating`, `levelling` (UK) / `leveling` (US).

**Grammar patterns:** `stand at + value` · `peak at + value` · `level off at + value` · `rise by + amount` · `rise to + final figure` · `account for + %` · `be twice as high as`

## 7. Band 8 accuracy checklist (your 3-minute check)

- [ ] Majority of sentences error-free; errors are **not** repeated patterns
- [ ] **Articles:** *the price of X*, *the figure*, *a rise of 10%*, *the highest figure*
- [ ] **Subject–verb agreement:** *the figure stands at...*, *the number of visitors was...*
- [ ] **Punctuation:** commas around contrasts, correct apostrophes, full stops clear
- [ ] **No first/second person, no contractions, no bullets**
- [ ] **No copied rubric wording**
- [ ] Every key feature has data support; every comparison is relevant
- [ ] Overview is present, clear, and contains no specific figures
- [ ] Word count 165–185

**Complex structures worth using (accurately!):**
- *Having peaked in 2008, the figure then declined slightly.*
- *While category A rose steadily, category B remained virtually unchanged.*
- *Although the early years saw only modest growth, the period 2006–2008 witnessed a dramatic surge.*

## 9. Worked micro-example (your line graph: 10 → 50, 2005–2010)

**Introduction**
> The line graph illustrates changes in the price of a commodity over the six-year period from 2005 to 2010.

**Overview**
> Overall, the price followed a consistently upward path, increasing fivefold from $10 to $50. Growth was most pronounced between 2006 and 2008, whereas the final two years saw only a gradual climb.

**Body 1**
> In the initial year, the price stood at $10, before edging up to roughly $15 in 2006. This modest early rise was followed by a marked acceleration: by 2007 the figure had reached $25, an increase of about 10 units in a single year.

**Body 2**
> The upward trend continued thereafter, with the price climbing to $41 in 2008 and then advancing more gradually to $44 in 2009. It peaked at $50 in 2010, having more than quadrupled since the mid-period, and closing the six years at five times its starting value.

## 10. Practice plan to Band 8–9

1. **Overview drills (10 a day):** for any chart, write 2 overview sentences only — trend + extremes/start-end. No figures.
2. **Paraphrase drills:** rewrite 5 prompts' introductions using a different reporting verb each time.
3. **Error log:** record your 3 most frequent systematic errors; do not write another essay until you can avoid them for 3 scripts in a row.
4. **Timed essays:** 2 per week, 20 minutes exactly; self-mark with §8 before checking with a teacher.
5. **Vocabulary:** learn collocations in chunks (*a sharp rise*, *rose sharply*, *a marginal increase*), never isolated words.
