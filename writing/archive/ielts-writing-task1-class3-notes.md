## 1. What this class added: the mixed-chart template

When a task gives **two different kinds of visual**, treat them as two separate information sets.

| Paragraph | Job | Template |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | Name both visual types and what each shows | *The provided [type 1] illustrates information regarding [main feature of type 1], while the [type 2] displays data about [main feature of type 2]. [Units].* |
| **Overview** | One big-picture point from each visual, linked with a contrast word | *Overall, it is evident that [main point of type 1], whereas [summary of type 2].* |
| **Body 1** | Describe **type 1** only | *Looking at the [type 1], it can be observed that...* |
| **Body 2** | Describe **type 2** only | *On the other hand, the [type 2] indicates that...* |

**Rules for mixed charts:**
- Keep each visual in **its own body paragraph** — do not mix table figures and pie percentages in one paragraph.
- Use `while` in the introduction and `whereas` in the overview to link the two visuals.
- State the **units for each visual** in the introduction (e.g. £m for the table, % for the pie charts).
- The overview still contains **no exact figures**.
- `On the other hand` is a safe body-2 opener when the two visuals show different kinds of information.

## 3. Teacher's model — upgraded to Band 8–9

**Introduction**
> The provided table illustrates information regarding the main sources of funding for a
> police department in a particular area of Britain, while the two pie charts display data
> about how its budget was allocated in 2017 and 2018. Funding figures are given in millions
> of pounds (£m), and expenditure is shown as a percentage (%).

**Overview**
> Overall, it is evident that the national government was the chief source of income for the
> police department in both years, whereas the majority of the budget was consistently
> allocated to the salaries of officers and staff.

**Body 1 — the table**
> Looking at the table, it can be observed that the total budget rose noticeably from
> £304.7 million in 2017 to £318.6 million in 2018. The primary contributor was the national
> government, providing approximately £175.5 million in the initial year and a slightly higher
> figure in 2018. Local taxes also increased, from £91.2 million to £102.7 million, while
> funding from other sources remained almost unchanged at roughly £38 million.

**Body 2 — the pie charts**
> On the other hand, the pie charts indicate that salaries of officers and staff accounted
> for the largest share of spending in both years, although the proportion fell from roughly
> three-quarters in 2017 to about three-fifths in 2018. The remainder of the budget was divided
> between technology and building and transport, with each category taking a noticeably larger
> share in 2018.

## 5. Reusable sentence bank for mixed charts

| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Introduction** | The provided [table / bar chart / line graph] illustrates information regarding... · while the [pie charts / table] display data about... · Figures are given in millions of pounds (£m) · expenditure is shown as a percentage (%) |
| **Overview** | Overall, it is evident that... · whereas... · while the other visual shows... · The main contrast is that... |
| **Body 1 — table** | Looking at the table, it can be observed that... · the total rose from X to Y · the primary contributor was... · remained almost unchanged at roughly... |
| **Body 2 — pie charts** | On the other hand, the pie charts indicate that... · accounted for the largest share · the proportion fell from... to... · the remainder was divided between... |
| **Comparison** | `whereas` (contrast between two visuals) · `while` (linking two facts) · `although` (concession inside a sentence) |

---

## 6. Band 8–9 checklist for mixed charts

- [ ] Both visual types named in the introduction, with units for each
- [ ] Overview covers one main point from **each** visual, linked with `while` / `whereas`
- [ ] Overview contains **no exact figures**
- [ ] Body 1 describes only the first visual; Body 2 only the second
- [ ] Key features selected and supported with data (£m for table, % for pie charts)
- [ ] Comparisons made — biggest source, biggest share, changes over time
- [ ] Approximations used where figures are read from a chart: `approximately · roughly · about`
- [ ] Word count 165–185
- [ ] Grammar: plurals (`pie charts`), articles (`the chief source`), prepositions (`in both years`)
